Question 1 · Biostatistics, epidemiology and research · medium

In a trial of a new statin, 1,000 patients were randomised to the statin and 1,000 to placebo. In the statin group, 150 stopped their tablets because of muscle aches, and 50 in the placebo group stopped for various reasons. In the main analysis, the investigators compare outcomes of all 1,000 patients in each group according to the group they were assigned to, whatever they actually took.

What is this type of analysis called?

  1. A. Per-protocol analysis
  2. B. As-treated analysis
  3. C. Subgroup analysis
  4. D. Intention-to-treat
  5. E. Interim analysis
Show the answer and explanation

Answer: D. Intention-to-treat

In an intention-to-treat (ITT) analysis, all randomised participants are analysed in the group to which they were allocated, regardless of whether they took the treatment, switched or stopped. This preserves the balance created by randomisation and reflects real-world effectiveness, because some patients will always stop treatment, often for reasons linked to the drug (such as side effects) or their prognosis. A per-protocol analysis includes only those who completed treatment as planned; it can show efficacy under ideal conditions but may be biased because those who stop are often different from those who continue. ITT is the main analysis for superiority trials.

Why the other options are wrong

  • A. Per-protocol analysis: Includes only those who followed the protocol.
  • B. As-treated analysis: Groups people by what they actually took.
  • C. Subgroup analysis: Looks at subsets of patients.
  • E. Interim analysis: Done partway through a trial.

Key point: Intention-to-treat analysis compares participants in the groups to which they were randomised, preserving randomisation.

Question 2 · Biostatistics, epidemiology and research · medium

In a 5-year trial in people with high cardiovascular risk, 20% of those on placebo had a heart attack or stroke, compared with 15% of those taking a new drug. The difference was statistically significant. A general practitioner wants to explain the benefit to her patients in a simple way.

What is the number needed to treat for 5 years to prevent one event?

  1. A. 5
  2. B. 20
  3. C. 25
  4. D. 75
  5. E. 133
Show the answer and explanation

Answer: B. 20

The absolute risk reduction (ARR) is the event rate in the control group minus that in the treatment group: 20% - 15% = 5% = 0.05. The number needed to treat (NNT) is 1/ARR = 1/0.05 = 20: on average, 20 people must take the drug for 5 years to prevent one heart attack or stroke. NNT always refers to a specific outcome and time period, and it should be rounded up to a whole number. It helps patients and doctors weigh benefit against cost and side effects, because it shows absolute benefit, whereas relative measures can make small benefits sound large.

Why the other options are wrong

  • A. 5: This is the absolute difference in percent, not the NNT.
  • C. 25: This is the relative risk reduction in percent.
  • D. 75: Not the correct calculation.
  • E. 133: Not the correct calculation.

Key point: NNT = 1/absolute risk reduction, for a stated outcome and time period.

Question 3 · Biostatistics, epidemiology and research · medium

In the same 5-year trial, heart attack or stroke occurred in 20% of patients on placebo and 15% of patients on the new drug. A drug company leaflet states the drug "reduces heart attacks and strokes by a quarter". A student wants to check this figure.

What is the relative risk reduction?

  1. A. 5%
  2. B. 15%
  3. C. 25%
  4. D. 33%
  5. E. 75%
Show the answer and explanation

Answer: C. 25%

The relative risk (RR) is the event rate in the treated group divided by that in the control group: 15%/20% = 0.75. The relative risk reduction (RRR) is 1 - RR = 0.25, or 25%; equivalently, ARR divided by the control event rate (5%/20%). The leaflet is correct that the drug reduces events "by a quarter", but this relative figure sounds impressive even if the absolute benefit is small. The absolute risk reduction (5%) and NNT (20) give a clearer picture of the real benefit for an individual. Patients should be given absolute figures, with time periods, when making decisions.

Why the other options are wrong

  • A. 5%: This is the absolute risk reduction.
  • B. 15%: This is the event rate on the drug.
  • D. 33%: 33% divides the 5% difference by 15%; the relative reduction is 5% divided by the placebo rate of 20%, which is 25%.
  • E. 75%: This is the relative risk.

Key point: Relative risk reduction = 1 - RR = ARR ÷ control event rate; absolute figures give a clearer picture of benefit.

Question 4 · Biostatistics, epidemiology and research · medium

In a trial of a new painkiller over 12 months, serious stomach bleeding occurred in 6% of patients taking the drug and in 2% of patients taking placebo. The difference was statistically significant. A pharmacist wants to describe this harm in a way that can be compared with the drug's benefit.

What is the number needed to harm over 12 months?

  1. A. 3
  2. B. 4
  3. C. 17
  4. D. 25
  5. E. 50
Show the answer and explanation

Answer: D. 25

The absolute risk increase (ARI) for the harm is 6% - 2% = 4% = 0.04. The number needed to harm (NNH) is 1/ARI = 1/0.04 = 25: on average, for every 25 people treated for 12 months, one extra person will have a serious bleed because of the drug. Comparing NNT for a benefit with NNH for a harm, over the same time period, helps decide whether a treatment is worthwhile for a given patient. The relative risk of bleeding is 6%/2% = 3, which again sounds alarming without the absolute figures. NNH, like NNT, is rounded and stated with its outcome and time period.

Why the other options are wrong

  • A. 3: This is the relative risk.
  • B. 4: This is the risk difference in percent.
  • C. 17: 17 is 1 divided by 6%; the number needed to harm is 1 divided by the difference, 4%, which is 25.
  • E. 50: 50 is 1 divided by 2%; the number needed to harm uses the 4% difference, which gives 25.

Key point: NNH = 1/absolute risk increase for a harm, over a stated time period.

Question 5 · Biostatistics, epidemiology and research · medium

A study compares 40 countries and finds that those with higher average dietary fat intake per person have higher breast cancer rates, with a strong correlation (r = 0.8). The data come from national food supply figures and national cancer registries. No information is available about what individual women with or without breast cancer ate.

What is the main limitation of this study?

  1. A. Recall bias
  2. B. Loss of participants to follow-up
  3. C. Ecological fallacy
  4. D. Lead-time bias
  5. E. Observer bias
Show the answer and explanation

Answer: C. Ecological fallacy

An ecological study uses data about groups or populations (countries, districts, years), not individuals. Such studies are quick, cheap and useful for generating hypotheses, using routinely collected data. Their main weakness is the ecological fallacy: an association seen at the group level may not hold for individuals, because we do not know whether the women who developed breast cancer were the ones who ate more fat. Country-level differences in other factors, such as wealth, age structure, childbearing, obesity, screening and registration, can also confound the comparison. Individual-level studies such as cohort or case-control studies are needed to test the hypothesis.

Why the other options are wrong

  • A. Recall bias: No individuals were asked.
  • B. Loss of participants to follow-up: This ecological study has no follow-up, so loss to follow-up cannot occur.
  • D. Lead-time bias: Lead-time bias applies to screening, not to comparisons between countries.
  • E. Observer bias: Observer bias is not the main problem; national averages cannot show that the individuals with high fat intake are the ones who get cancer.

Key point: Ecological studies compare groups; their main limitation is the ecological fallacy.

Question 6 · Biostatistics, epidemiology and research · medium

A meta-analysis of 8 randomised trials examines whether a vitamin supplement reduces preterm birth. The forest plot shows each trial as a square with a horizontal line, and a diamond at the bottom for the pooled result. The pooled risk ratio is 0.92, and the diamond stretches from 0.81 to 1.05, crossing the vertical line drawn at 1.

What does the pooled result show?

  1. A. No significant overall effect
  2. B. A significant 8% reduction
  3. C. A significant increase in preterm birth
  4. D. That all trials agree
  5. E. Publication bias
Show the answer and explanation

Answer: A. No significant overall effect

A forest plot shows each study's estimate (square, sized by its weight) and 95% confidence interval (horizontal line), with a vertical line of no effect (1 for ratios such as risk ratio and odds ratio, 0 for differences). The diamond represents the pooled estimate: its centre is the point estimate and its width the confidence interval. If the diamond crosses the line of no effect, the pooled result is not statistically significant at the 5% level. Here the risk ratio of 0.92 suggests a possible 8% reduction, but the interval (0.81-1.05) is compatible with anything from a 19% reduction to a 5% increase. Heterogeneity (I² statistic) shows how consistent the trials are.

Why the other options are wrong

  • B. A significant 8% reduction: The confidence interval includes 1, so the 8% reduction is not statistically significant.
  • C. A significant increase in preterm birth: The point estimate is below 1.
  • D. That all trials agree: Agreement is shown by heterogeneity tests.
  • E. Publication bias: Publication bias is shown by a funnel plot, not by the diamond of a forest plot.

Key point: On a forest plot, a pooled diamond crossing the line of no effect (1 for ratios) means no statistically significant effect.

Question 7 · Biostatistics, epidemiology and research · medium

A systematic review of 20 trials of a herbal remedy for knee pain plots each trial's effect size against its precision (sample size). The large trials cluster around a small benefit near no effect. Small trials showing large benefits are present, but small trials showing no benefit or harm are almost completely missing from the lower left of the plot.

What does this pattern most likely suggest?

  1. A. Recall bias
  2. B. Lead-time bias
  3. C. Perfect agreement among trials
  4. D. Confounding by age
  5. E. Publication bias
Show the answer and explanation

Answer: E. Publication bias

A funnel plot plots each study's effect against a measure of its precision. Without bias, small studies scatter widely around the true effect and large studies cluster near it, forming a symmetrical inverted funnel. Asymmetry, with small studies showing no effect or harm missing, suggests publication bias: positive results are more likely to be published, published in English, or published quickly, while small negative studies stay in drawers. Other causes of asymmetry include poor-quality small studies and true differences between small and large studies. Searching trial registries and unpublished data, and statistical tests such as Egger's, help detect it. Publication bias exaggerates pooled effects.

Why the other options are wrong

  • A. Recall bias: Applies to case-control studies.
  • B. Lead-time bias: Lead-time bias applies to screening.
  • C. Perfect agreement among trials: An asymmetric funnel plot shows disagreement between small and large trials, not perfect agreement.
  • D. Confounding by age: Not shown by a funnel plot.

Key point: Funnel plot asymmetry with missing small negative studies suggests publication bias.

Question 8 · Biostatistics, epidemiology and research · medium

A new drug for a chronic kidney disease does not cure it but keeps patients alive for an average of 10 years instead of 4. Over the next decade, the number of new cases diagnosed each year in a district stays the same. The district health officer is surprised that the number of people living with the disease keeps rising.

What explains the rise?

  1. A. Incidence has risen
  2. B. The drug causes the disease
  3. C. Diagnosis has become less accurate
  4. D. Longer duration of disease
  5. E. Patients are being cured faster
Show the answer and explanation

Answer: D. Longer duration of disease

In a steady state, prevalence is approximately incidence multiplied by the average duration of disease (P ≈ I × D, for a fairly rare condition). Prevalence increases when new cases increase, when patients live longer with the disease, or when people with the disease move into the area; it decreases when patients recover faster or die sooner, or when incidence falls. A treatment that prolongs survival without cure therefore raises prevalence even if incidence is unchanged, which is a sign of success, not failure, but increases the need for services. Conversely, a disease that kills quickly has a low prevalence despite a high incidence.

Why the other options are wrong

  • A. Incidence has risen: New cases are stable, so incidence has not risen; prevalence rises because patients live longer.
  • B. The drug causes the disease: Nothing suggests the drug causes the disease.
  • C. Diagnosis has become less accurate: Nothing suggests diagnosis has become less accurate.
  • E. Patients are being cured faster: The drug does not cure; patients live longer with the disease, so prevalence rises.

Key point: Prevalence ≈ incidence × duration; prolonging survival without cure increases prevalence.

Question 9 · Biostatistics, epidemiology and research · medium

A village has 10,000 residents at the start of the year. A survey on 1 January finds that 200 already have chronic hepatitis. All 10,000 are followed for the whole year, and 98 new cases of chronic hepatitis are diagnosed during the year among people who did not have it on 1 January. Nobody moves in or out, and nobody dies.

What is the incidence of chronic hepatitis for the year?

  1. A. 9.8 per 1,000
  2. B. 10 per 1,000
  3. C. 20 per 1,000
  4. D. 29.8 per 1,000
  5. E. 49 per 1,000
Show the answer and explanation

Answer: B. 10 per 1,000

Incidence (cumulative incidence or risk) is the number of new cases during a period divided by the population at risk at the start of that period. People who already have the disease are not at risk of becoming new cases, so they are removed from the denominator: 10,000 - 200 = 9,800. Incidence = 98/9,800 = 0.01, or 10 per 1,000 per year. The prevalence on 1 January was 200/10,000 = 20 per 1,000, and at the end of the year (if nobody recovered or died) it would be 298/10,000. When people are followed for different lengths of time, incidence rate uses person-time in the denominator instead.

Why the other options are wrong

  • A. 9.8 per 1,000: Uses the whole population as the denominator.
  • C. 20 per 1,000: This is the prevalence on 1 January.
  • D. 29.8 per 1,000: This adds old and new cases.
  • E. 49 per 1,000: Not the correct calculation.

Key point: Incidence = new cases ÷ population at risk (excluding existing cases) over a defined period.

Question 10 · Biostatistics, epidemiology and research · medium

In a province, about 3,000 new cases of a highly fatal cancer are diagnosed every year, but at any given time only about 1,500 people are living with it, because most patients die within 6 months of diagnosis. In the same province, about 1,000 new cases of a slowly progressive skin disease are diagnosed each year, but 20,000 people are living with it.

Why is the prevalence of the cancer low despite its high incidence?

  1. A. Its duration is short
  2. B. Its incidence is falling
  3. C. It is under-diagnosed
  4. D. People move away
  5. E. It is curable
Show the answer and explanation

Answer: A. Its duration is short

Prevalence depends on both incidence and duration (P ≈ I × D). A highly fatal disease with a short average duration has a low prevalence even if many new cases arise each year (here 3,000 per year × 0.5 year ≈ 1,500), while a chronic, non-fatal disease with long duration has a high prevalence even with fewer new cases (1,000 per year × 20 years ≈ 20,000). This matters for planning: prevalence shows the burden of people needing ongoing care (useful for chronic disease services), while incidence shows the rate of new disease and is used to study causes and the effect of prevention.

Why the other options are wrong

  • B. Its incidence is falling: Nothing says incidence is falling; prevalence is low because patients die quickly.
  • C. It is under-diagnosed: Nothing suggests under-diagnosis.
  • D. People move away: Nothing suggests migration.
  • E. It is curable: It is highly fatal, not curable; short duration keeps prevalence low.

Key point: A highly fatal disease has low prevalence despite high incidence because its duration is short.
