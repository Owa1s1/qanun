Question 1 · Community medicine and public health · easy

A new vaccinator at a basic health unit is learning to manage the ice-lined refrigerator. The supervisor checks the thermometer readings recorded twice a day for the past month and explains the correct storage range.

At what temperature should most vaccines be kept at a health facility?

  1. A. -25 °C to -15 °C
  2. B. 0 °C to +2 °C
  3. C. +2 °C to +8 °C
  4. D. +10 °C to +15 °C
  5. E. Room temperature
Show the answer and explanation

Answer: C. +2 °C to +8 °C

The cold chain is the system of people, equipment and procedures used to keep vaccines at the correct temperature from manufacture to the point of use. At health facilities, all EPI vaccines are stored between +2 °C and +8 °C. OPV can also be stored frozen (-25 °C to -15 °C) at national and provincial stores. Temperatures should be recorded twice daily. Ice-lined refrigerators maintain safe temperatures during power cuts. Vaccines are carried to outreach sessions in cold boxes and vaccine carriers with conditioned ice packs. Both heat and freezing can damage vaccines, so they must be protected from both.

Why the other options are wrong

  • A. -25 °C to -15 °C: For OPV in central stores only.
  • B. 0 °C to +2 °C: Too cold; risk of freezing.
  • D. +10 °C to +15 °C: +10 to +15 °C is too warm; vaccines are kept at +2 to +8 °C.
  • E. Room temperature: Room temperature is too warm for vaccine storage.

Key point: Vaccines are kept at +2 °C to +8 °C at health facilities.

Question 2 · Community medicine and public health · easy

A government health committee in Pakistan wants to reduce smoking, especially among young people and the poor. It is considering several policies included in the WHO MPOWER package, and wants the single measure shown to be most effective in reducing tobacco use.

Which measure is most effective?

  1. A. Printing leaflets for schools
  2. B. Raising taxes
  3. C. Holding an annual awareness walk
  4. D. Building more cancer hospitals
  5. E. Voluntary advertising codes for the industry
Show the answer and explanation

Answer: B. Raising taxes

The WHO MPOWER package helps countries implement the Framework Convention on Tobacco Control: Monitor tobacco use and prevention policies, Protect people from tobacco smoke, Offer help to quit, Warn about the dangers (pictorial health warnings, mass media), Enforce bans on advertising, promotion and sponsorship, and Raise taxes on tobacco. Raising taxes, and therefore prices, is the single most effective measure to reduce tobacco use, particularly among young people and those on low incomes, who are more sensitive to price. It also raises government revenue. Selling loose single cigarettes undermines price measures and is banned in Pakistan.

Why the other options are wrong

  • A. Printing leaflets for schools: Leaflets alone have much less effect than price rises.
  • C. Holding an annual awareness walk: An awareness walk has little effect on its own.
  • D. Building more cancer hospitals: Treatment, not prevention.
  • E. Voluntary advertising codes for the industry: Voluntary codes are widely evaded; raising the price through tax is the single most effective measure.

Key point: Raising tobacco taxes is the single most effective measure to reduce tobacco use.

Question 3 · Community medicine and public health · medium

In planning the expansion of the Lady Health Worker programme into remote districts of Balochistan, officials insist that each new LHW must be a woman living in the community she will serve, with a minimum level of school education, rather than recruiting qualified women from cities. A student asks why this rule is so important.

What is the main reason for recruiting from the community?

  1. A. She is accepted and trusted locally
  2. B. She can work without supervision
  3. C. Her salary is lower than a nurse's
  4. D. She needs less training than an outsider
  5. E. She can replace the local health facility
Show the answer and explanation

Answer: A. She is accepted and trusted locally

In many parts of Pakistan, cultural norms limit women's movement outside the home and their contact with unrelated men. A Lady Health Worker who belongs to the community is known and trusted, speaks the local language, understands local customs and can visit women in their homes, which outside workers or male health staff cannot easily do. She is available day to day, and her own home becomes a health house. This reflects the primary health care principles of community participation and appropriate, acceptable services. LHWs receive initial and refresher training, supplies and supervision from Lady Health Supervisors attached to health facilities.

Why the other options are wrong

  • B. She can work without supervision: She is supervised by a Lady Health Supervisor and linked to a health facility.
  • C. Her salary is lower than a nurse's: Cost is not the reason; acceptance by the households she visits is.
  • D. She needs less training than an outsider: She receives the same structured training, about 15 months, wherever she comes from.
  • E. She can replace the local health facility: She links households to the facility and refers to it; she does not replace it.

Key point: LHWs are recruited from their own communities so they are trusted and can reach women at home.

Question 4 · Community medicine and public health · medium

A national survey in Pakistan asks married women aged 15-49 about their fertility wishes and contraceptive use. A substantial proportion say they want to wait at least 2 years before their next child or want no more children at all, yet they are not using any method of contraception, often because of fear of side effects, family opposition or lack of access.

What does this group of women represent?

  1. A. Unmet need
  2. B. Contraceptive prevalence
  3. C. Total fertility rate
  4. D. Secondary infertility
  5. E. Demographic dividend
Show the answer and explanation

Answer: A. Unmet need

Unmet need for family planning is the proportion of women of reproductive age who are fecund and sexually active, want to delay their next birth by at least 2 years (unmet need for spacing) or want no more children (unmet need for limiting), but are not using any contraceptive method. In Pakistan, unmet need is high, while contraceptive prevalence has risen only slowly. Reasons include fear of side effects, husband or family opposition, religious misconceptions, poor access and poor quality counselling. Meeting this need through accessible services, Lady Health Workers, community and religious leader engagement and a wide choice of methods would reduce unintended pregnancies, unsafe abortions and maternal deaths.

Why the other options are wrong

  • B. Contraceptive prevalence: The proportion using contraception.
  • C. Total fertility rate: Average number of children per woman.
  • D. Secondary infertility: Not the situation described.
  • E. Demographic dividend: An economic effect of falling fertility.

Key point: Unmet need is the proportion of women who want to space or limit births but use no contraception.

Question 5 · Community medicine and public health · medium

National surveillance in Pakistan shows HIV prevalence above 5% among people who inject drugs and in some other key populations, while prevalence among pregnant women attending antenatal clinics remains below 1%.

How is this epidemic classified?

  1. A. Low-level
  2. B. Concentrated
  3. C. Generalised
  4. D. Eliminated
  5. E. Pandemic only
Show the answer and explanation

Answer: B. Concentrated

WHO and UNAIDS classify HIV epidemics as low-level (prevalence below 5% in all key populations), concentrated (5% or more in at least one key population, but below 1% among pregnant women in urban areas) or generalised (1% or more in the general population). Pakistan has a concentrated epidemic, with high prevalence among people who inject drugs, transgender people and male and female sex workers, which can spread to their partners and children. Outbreaks linked to unsafe medical injections, such as the 2019 outbreak among children in Larkana, show the risk to the general population. Prevention focuses on key populations, harm reduction, testing, treatment and safe injections.

Why the other options are wrong

  • A. Low-level: Prevalence is above 5% in a key group.
  • C. Generalised: Would need 1% or more in the general population.
  • D. Eliminated: HIV has not been eliminated in Pakistan.
  • E. Pandemic only: "Pandemic only" is not a WHO category for a national HIV epidemic.

Key point: Pakistan has a concentrated HIV epidemic: 5% or more in key populations but below 1% in the general population.

Question 6 · Community medicine and public health · medium

A 24-year-old pregnant woman is found to be HIV positive at her first antenatal visit at 16 weeks. She is well. She wants to know how to protect her baby from infection.

What is the most effective measure?

  1. A. Termination of pregnancy
  2. B. Vitamin supplements
  3. C. Treatment only after delivery
  4. D. Lifelong ART
  5. E. Antibiotics during labour
Show the answer and explanation

Answer: D. Lifelong ART

Without intervention, about 15-45% of babies born to mothers with HIV become infected during pregnancy, delivery or breastfeeding. WHO recommends that all pregnant and breastfeeding women with HIV start lifelong antiretroviral therapy (ART) as soon as possible, regardless of CD4 count. Suppressing the viral load reduces transmission to well below 5% (and below 1% with sustained suppression). The baby also receives antiretroviral prophylaxis after birth, and infants are tested early. With maternal ART, breastfeeding is supported where formula feeding is not safe. Testing all pregnant women for HIV, syphilis and hepatitis B is part of the triple elimination strategy.

Why the other options are wrong

  • A. Termination of pregnancy: Not needed to prevent transmission.
  • B. Vitamin supplements: Do not prevent transmission.
  • C. Treatment only after delivery: Treatment only after delivery is too late; antiretrovirals in pregnancy prevent most transmission.
  • E. Antibiotics during labour: Do not prevent HIV transmission.

Key point: Lifelong ART for pregnant women with HIV, with infant prophylaxis, prevents most mother-to-child transmission.

Question 7 · Community medicine and public health · medium

Many children and adults in a refugee settlement near Quetta develop painless skin ulcers on the face and arms that last for months and heal with scarring. The ulcers follow bites from tiny insects that are active at night. Health workers plan control measures.

Which vector control measure is most useful?

  1. A. Removing water containers
  2. B. Treated nets and spraying
  3. C. Chlorinating water
  4. D. Killing rats in the camp
  5. E. Larvicides in ponds
Show the answer and explanation

Answer: B. Treated nets and spraying

Cutaneous leishmaniasis is common in Pakistan, especially in Balochistan, Khyber Pakhtunkhwa and among displaced populations. It is transmitted by the bite of female sandflies, which are very small, bite mainly at night and breed in cracks in walls, rubble and animal shelters. Prevention includes insecticide-treated nets (sandflies can pass through ordinary nets, so treated or fine-mesh nets are needed), indoor residual spraying, insect repellents, improving housing and environmental management, and early diagnosis and treatment. Visceral leishmaniasis (kala-azar) occurs in some northern areas, where dogs are a reservoir.

Why the other options are wrong

  • A. Removing water containers: Removing water containers controls Aedes mosquitoes, not sandflies.
  • C. Chlorinating water: Chlorinating water prevents water-borne disease, not leishmaniasis.
  • D. Killing rats in the camp: For plague or leptospirosis.
  • E. Larvicides in ponds: Larvicides in ponds target mosquitoes; sandflies breed in cracks and rubble.

Key point: Cutaneous leishmaniasis spreads by sandflies; control uses insecticide-treated nets and residual spraying.

Question 8 · Community medicine and public health · medium

After the 2022 floods in Pakistan, which submerged large parts of Sindh and Balochistan, health teams in the affected districts saw a sharp rise in certain diseases in the following weeks and months among people living near standing water.

Which disease rose most after the floods?

  1. A. Tuberculosis
  2. B. Hepatitis B
  3. C. Rabies
  4. D. Poliomyelitis
  5. E. Malaria
Show the answer and explanation

Answer: E. Malaria

Climate change is increasing extreme weather events in Pakistan, including heat waves, droughts and floods. The 2022 floods affected over 30 million people. After floods, health risks include drowning and injuries, water-borne diseases (diarrhoea, cholera, typhoid, hepatitis A and E), vector-borne diseases (malaria and dengue, as stagnant water creates breeding sites), skin and eye infections, respiratory infections in crowded camps, snakebites, malnutrition and mental health problems. Malaria cases rose sharply in Sindh and Balochistan after the 2022 floods. Health responses include safe water, sanitation, vector control, distribution of nets, rapid testing and treatment, and disease surveillance.

Why the other options are wrong

  • A. Tuberculosis: TB services were disrupted, but there was no sharp rise within weeks.
  • B. Hepatitis B: Hepatitis B spreads by blood and is not linked to standing water.
  • C. Rabies: Rabies follows animal bites and did not surge with standing water.
  • D. Poliomyelitis: Polio spreads by the faecal-oral route; the sharp rise near standing water was in mosquito-borne disease.

Key point: After floods, malaria, dengue and water-borne diseases increase, requiring vector control and safe water.

Question 9 · Community medicine and public health · easy

A district health officer in Punjab is reviewing the services offered at basic health units. One of them is giving hepatitis B vaccine to every newborn within 24 hours of birth, before any infection has occurred, so that the children never become infected or develop chronic liver disease later in life.

Which level of prevention is this?

  1. A. Primary prevention
  2. B. Primordial prevention
  3. C. Secondary prevention
  4. D. Tertiary prevention
  5. E. Quaternary prevention
Show the answer and explanation

Answer: A. Primary prevention

The levels of prevention correspond to stages in the natural history of disease. Primordial prevention stops risk factors appearing in a population (for example, healthy lifestyles in children). Primary prevention acts before disease occurs, through health promotion (education, nutrition, housing) and specific protection (immunisation, chemoprophylaxis, iodised salt, helmets). Secondary prevention detects and treats disease early, before symptoms or complications (screening, case finding). Tertiary prevention reduces disability and complications of established disease (rehabilitation). Quaternary prevention protects patients from overmedicalisation. Vaccination is the classic example of specific protection, a form of primary prevention.

Why the other options are wrong

  • B. Primordial prevention: Prevents risk factors from arising in a population.
  • C. Secondary prevention: Early detection and treatment of existing disease.
  • D. Tertiary prevention: Limits disability from established disease.
  • E. Quaternary prevention: Protects patients from unnecessary medical harm.

Key point: Immunisation is specific protection, a form of primary prevention.

Question 10 · Community medicine and public health · easy

A women's health project in Lahore invites women aged 30-49 who feel completely well to have visual inspection of the cervix after acetic acid. Women with abnormal areas are treated the same day with cryotherapy or thermal ablation, before invasive cancer has developed.

Which level of prevention does this programme represent?

  1. A. Secondary prevention
  2. B. Primordial prevention
  3. C. Primary prevention
  4. D. Tertiary prevention
  5. E. Health promotion
Show the answer and explanation

Answer: A. Secondary prevention

Secondary prevention aims to detect disease at an early, often symptomless stage, and treat it to stop progression or cure it. Screening programmes (cervical screening, mammography, blood pressure checks, newborn screening) and case finding are secondary prevention. Treating precancerous cervical lesions found by screening prevents invasive cancer. Primary prevention of cervical cancer is HPV vaccination and safer sexual practices. Tertiary prevention would be treatment and rehabilitation of women with established cancer to reduce complications and improve quality of life. The "screen and treat" approach suits low-resource settings by reducing loss to follow-up.

Why the other options are wrong

  • B. Primordial prevention: Prevents risk factors arising.
  • C. Primary prevention: Would be HPV vaccination.
  • D. Tertiary prevention: Treats established disease to limit disability.
  • E. Health promotion: A part of primary prevention.

Key point: Screening and early treatment of precancerous or symptomless disease is secondary prevention.
