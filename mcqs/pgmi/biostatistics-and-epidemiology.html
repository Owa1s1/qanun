Question 1 · Biostatistics, epidemiology and research · medium

A rapid TB test was evaluated against culture in 1,000 people with cough: 200 had TB, of whom 160 tested positive; 800 did not have TB, of whom 760 tested negative and 40 tested positive. The programme manager is worried about people without TB who would be started on treatment unnecessarily.

What proportion of people without TB test falsely positive?

  1. A. 4%
  2. B. 5%
  3. C. 10%
  4. D. 20%
  5. E. 95%
Show the answer and explanation

Answer: B. 5%

The false positive rate is the proportion of people without the disease who test positive: false positives/(false positives + true negatives) = 40/800 = 5%. It equals 1 - specificity (specificity here is 760/800 = 95%). This differs from the proportion of positive results that are false (40 of 200 positives = 20%), which is 1 - positive predictive value and depends on the prevalence of disease. In a TB programme, false positives mean unnecessary treatment with its side effects and costs, which is why positive rapid tests may be confirmed or interpreted with clinical findings, especially where prevalence is low.

Why the other options are wrong

  • A. 4%: Uses the wrong denominator.
  • C. 10%: Not the correct calculation.
  • D. 20%: This is the proportion of all positives that are false.
  • E. 95%: 95% is the specificity (760/800); the false-positive rate is 1 - specificity, 40/800 = 5%.

Key point: False positive rate = false positives ÷ all without disease = 1 - specificity.

Question 2 · Biostatistics, epidemiology and research · medium

Four blood tests for sepsis in newborns are compared using receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves, plotting sensitivity against 1 - specificity across all possible cut-offs. The areas under the curve are: test A 0.52, test B 0.68, test C 0.75 and test D 0.92. A fifth test, E, has a curve lying along the diagonal line of the graph.

Which test discriminates best between babies with and without sepsis?

  1. A. Test A
  2. B. Test B
  3. C. Test C
  4. D. Test D
  5. E. Test E
Show the answer and explanation

Answer: D. Test D

A receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve plots the true positive rate (sensitivity) against the false positive rate (1 - specificity) for every possible cut-off of a test. A perfect test reaches the top-left corner (100% sensitivity, 0% false positives). A test no better than chance lies along the diagonal, with an area under the curve (AUC) of 0.5. The AUC summarises overall discrimination: 1.0 is perfect, above 0.9 excellent, 0.7-0.8 acceptable to good, and near 0.5 useless. The best cut-off is often the point closest to the top-left corner, though the choice depends on the costs of false positives and false negatives.

Why the other options are wrong

  • A. Test A: An area of 0.52 is close to chance.
  • B. Test B: Test B discriminates poorly.
  • C. Test C: Test C discriminates only fairly.
  • E. Test E: An area of 0.5 or less is no better than chance.

Key point: On an ROC curve, a larger area under the curve means better discrimination; 0.5 is chance.

Question 3 · Biostatistics, epidemiology and research · medium

A random sample of 144 adults from a district has a mean systolic blood pressure of 132 mmHg with a standard deviation of 12 mmHg. The researchers want to describe how precisely this sample mean estimates the true mean systolic pressure of all adults in the district.

What is the standard error of the mean?

  1. A. 0.08 mmHg
  2. B. 1 mmHg
  3. C. 11 mmHg
  4. D. 12 mmHg
  5. E. 144 mmHg
Show the answer and explanation

Answer: B. 1 mmHg

The standard error (SE) of the mean measures how precisely a sample mean estimates the population mean. It equals the standard deviation divided by the square root of the sample size: SE = SD/√n = 12/√144 = 12/12 = 1 mmHg. The SE becomes smaller as the sample size increases, because larger samples give more precise estimates. It is used to calculate confidence intervals: the 95% CI for the mean is about mean ± 1.96 SE, here 132 ± 2, or about 130 to 134 mmHg. The SD (12 mmHg) describes how much individual blood pressures vary; the SE describes the uncertainty in the mean.

Why the other options are wrong

  • A. 0.08 mmHg: This is 12 divided by 144.
  • C. 11 mmHg: Not the correct calculation.
  • D. 12 mmHg: This is the standard deviation.
  • E. 144 mmHg: 144 is the variance (in mmHg squared); the standard error is 12 divided by the square root of 144, which is 1 mmHg.

Key point: Standard error of the mean = SD ÷ √n; it measures the precision of the sample mean.

Question 4 · Biostatistics, epidemiology and research · medium

A survey of monthly household income in a city in Punjab finds that most households earn modest amounts, but a small number of very wealthy households earn many times more, giving a long tail to the right on the histogram. The researchers calculate the mean, median and mode of household income.

How are these measures most likely to be arranged?

  1. A. Mean > median > mode
  2. B. Mode > median > mean
  3. C. Mean = median = mode
  4. D. Median > mean > mode
  5. E. Mode > mean > median
Show the answer and explanation

Answer: A. Mean > median > mode

In a positively (right) skewed distribution, a few very large values form a long tail to the right. The mean is pulled towards the tail, so it is larger than the median, which in turn is usually larger than the mode (the peak): mean > median > mode. Income, hospital stay, triglycerides, many hormone levels and survival times are typically right-skewed. In a negatively (left) skewed distribution, with a long tail to the left, the order is reversed: mean < median < mode. In a symmetrical distribution such as the normal, all three are equal. The median is usually the better summary for skewed data.

Why the other options are wrong

  • B. Mode > median > mean: Seen in negative (left) skew.
  • C. Mean = median = mode: Seen in symmetrical distributions.
  • D. Median > mean > mode: With a right skew, the mean is pulled above the median, not below it.
  • E. Mode > mean > median: With a right skew, the mode is lowest, not highest.

Key point: In positively skewed data, mean > median > mode; in negatively skewed data, the order is reversed.

Question 5 · Biostatistics, epidemiology and research · medium

A trial of a new drug in heart failure follows patients for an average of 2 years and reports a hazard ratio for death of 0.60 (95% CI 0.48 to 0.75) for the new drug compared with standard care, from a Cox proportional hazards model. A cardiologist explains the result to a patient.

What does a hazard ratio of 0.60 mean?

  1. A. The drug prevents 60% of deaths
  2. B. 60% of patients survive
  3. C. The drug adds 0.6 years of life
  4. D. The result is not significant
  5. E. 40% lower hazard at any time
Show the answer and explanation

Answer: E. 40% lower hazard at any time

The hazard is the instantaneous rate of the event (here death) among people still at risk at a given time. A hazard ratio compares the hazard in one group with another, assuming the ratio is roughly constant over time (proportional hazards). A hazard ratio of 0.60 means that at any point during follow-up, patients on the new drug had a 40% lower rate of death than those on standard care. Because the 95% CI (0.48 to 0.75) excludes 1, the result is statistically significant. Hazard ratios come from Cox regression or log-rank-type analyses and, like relative risks, should be accompanied by absolute measures for decision-making.

Why the other options are wrong

  • A. The drug prevents 60% of deaths: A hazard ratio of 0.60 means a 40% lower hazard of death, not 60% of deaths prevented.
  • B. 60% of patients survive: Not what a hazard ratio measures.
  • C. The drug adds 0.6 years of life: Not what a hazard ratio measures.
  • D. The result is not significant: The interval excludes 1, so the result is significant.

Key point: A hazard ratio of 0.60 means a 40% lower instantaneous event rate at any time during follow-up.

Question 6 · Biostatistics, epidemiology and research · easy

A research team visits randomly chosen households in Lahore during one week and tests 1,200 adults for diabetes with HbA1c, at the same time recording their weight, height and diet. They find that 180 of the 1,200 adults have diabetes. No one is followed up afterwards. The team reports the figure to the provincial health department.

What does the figure of 180 out of 1,200 measure?

  1. A. An incidence of 15%
  2. B. A relative risk of 1.5
  3. C. A prevalence of 15%
  4. D. An attack rate of 15%
  5. E. A mortality rate of 15%
Show the answer and explanation

Answer: C. A prevalence of 15%

A cross-sectional study measures exposure and outcome in a population at a single point (or short period) in time, like a snapshot. It measures prevalence: the proportion of people who have the condition at that time, here 180/1,200 = 15%. Incidence, in contrast, counts new cases arising in people who were free of the disease, followed over a period of time, which needs a cohort design. Cross-sectional surveys are quick and relatively cheap and are useful for planning services (such as how many people need diabetes care), but because exposure and outcome are measured together, they cannot show which came first.

Why the other options are wrong

  • A. An incidence of 15%: Incidence needs new cases over time.
  • B. A relative risk of 1.5: Needs two compared groups.
  • D. An attack rate of 15%: Used for outbreaks over a short period.
  • E. A mortality rate of 15%: Counts deaths, not cases.

Key point: A cross-sectional survey measures prevalence: existing cases divided by the population at one point in time.

Question 7 · Biostatistics, epidemiology and research · easy

Researchers at a cancer hospital in Peshawar enrol 100 patients newly diagnosed with oral squamous cell carcinoma and 200 people of similar age and sex without cancer from the same outpatient department. Trained interviewers ask everyone about their past use of naswar, gutka, cigarettes and alcohol over the previous 20 years. The two groups are then compared.

What type of study is this?

  1. A. Cohort study
  2. B. Cross-sectional study
  3. C. Randomised controlled trial
  4. D. Ecological study
  5. E. Case-control study
Show the answer and explanation

Answer: E. Case-control study

A case-control study starts with people who have the outcome (cases) and a comparison group without it (controls), then looks back to compare how often each group was exposed to possible causes. It is efficient for rare diseases and diseases with long latency, such as cancers, and can study several exposures at once. Its results are expressed as an odds ratio. Its main weaknesses are recall bias (cases may remember exposures differently), difficulty choosing suitable controls (selection bias), and the inability to measure incidence or relative risk directly, because the investigator chooses how many cases and controls to include.

Why the other options are wrong

  • A. Cohort study: Would start with exposure and follow forwards.
  • B. Cross-sectional study: Would sample people regardless of disease.
  • C. Randomised controlled trial: Exposure is not assigned.
  • D. Ecological study: Uses individuals, not population averages.

Key point: A case-control study compares past exposures between people with and without a disease; it suits rare diseases.

Question 8 · Biostatistics, epidemiology and research · easy

Researchers record the smoking habits of 5,000 workers at a textile factory in Faisalabad, none of whom has tuberculosis at the start. They examine all workers every year for 10 years and record new cases of tuberculosis in smokers and non-smokers. They then compare how often new disease developed in each group.

What type of study is this?

  1. A. Prospective cohort study
  2. B. Case-control study
  3. C. Cross-sectional study
  4. D. Randomised controlled trial
  5. E. Ecological study
Show the answer and explanation

Answer: A. Prospective cohort study

A cohort study starts with people free of the outcome, classifies them by exposure (here smoking), and follows them over time to compare the incidence of new disease in exposed and unexposed groups. A prospective cohort follows people forwards from now; a retrospective (historical) cohort uses existing records to look back at a group defined in the past and trace outcomes up to the present. Cohort studies establish temporality, measure incidence and relative risk directly, and can study several outcomes of one exposure, but they need large numbers and long follow-up for rare diseases and are vulnerable to loss to follow-up.

Why the other options are wrong

  • B. Case-control study: Would start from people with TB.
  • C. Cross-sectional study: Would measure both at one time.
  • D. Randomised controlled trial: Smoking was not assigned.
  • E. Ecological study: Uses individual data here.

Key point: A cohort study follows people grouped by exposure over time and compares the incidence of new disease.

Question 9 · Biostatistics, epidemiology and research · easy

A new oral diabetes drug was approved after trials in about 5,000 patients showed it lowered HbA1c safely. Two years after it went on sale and was used by over a million people, reports to the national pharmacovigilance centre reveal a rare form of liver failure occurring in about 1 in 50,000 users.

In which phase of drug development was this side effect detected?

  1. A. Phase I
  2. B. Phase IV
  3. C. Phase II
  4. D. Phase III
  5. E. Preclinical testing
Show the answer and explanation

Answer: B. Phase IV

Drug development moves through preclinical (laboratory and animal) studies, then phase I (small numbers of healthy volunteers or patients, testing safety, tolerability and pharmacokinetics), phase II (a few hundred patients, testing efficacy and dosing), and phase III (large randomised trials, often thousands of patients, comparing the drug with placebo or standard treatment before licensing). Even large phase III trials cannot reliably detect adverse effects rarer than about 1 in several thousand. Phase IV is post-marketing surveillance after approval, through spontaneous reporting (such as yellow card or national pharmacovigilance systems), registries and observational studies, which detects rare and long-term effects.

Why the other options are wrong

  • A. Phase I: First-in-human studies in small numbers.
  • C. Phase II: Small studies of efficacy and dosing.
  • D. Phase III: Large trials before approval.
  • E. Preclinical testing: Done in the laboratory and animals.

Key point: Rare adverse drug effects are usually detected in phase IV post-marketing surveillance.

Question 10 · Biostatistics, epidemiology and research · easy

After a wedding dinner in a village near Hyderabad, 400 guests are known to have eaten. Within 48 hours, 120 of them develop watery diarrhoea and vomiting. The district health team interviews all 400 guests about what they ate. No illness occurs among people who did not attend.

What is the overall attack rate among the guests?

  1. A. 3%
  2. B. 12%
  3. C. 30%
  4. D. 33%
  5. E. 40%
Show the answer and explanation

Answer: C. 30%

The attack rate is the proportion of people exposed in an outbreak who become ill during a short, defined period: 120/400 = 0.30, or 30%. It is a form of cumulative incidence used in outbreaks. In a food-borne outbreak, food-specific attack rates are then calculated for those who did and did not eat each dish; the food with the greatest difference (highest relative risk) is the likely source. The secondary attack rate measures spread to contacts of primary cases, such as household members, and reflects how infectious a disease is. Investigation also includes an epidemic curve, sampling of food and water, and control measures.

Why the other options are wrong

  • A. 3%: Not the correct calculation.
  • B. 12%: Not the correct calculation.
  • D. 33%: Not the correct calculation.
  • E. 40%: Not the correct calculation.

Key point: Attack rate = number ill ÷ number exposed in an outbreak; food-specific attack rates identify the source.
