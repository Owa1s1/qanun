Question 1 · English language (PGMI) · medium

Because the senior registrar was delayed in the operating theatre, she asked her colleague to _______ for her during the afternoon outpatient clinic.

Which phrasal verb best completes the sentence?

  1. A. stand in
  2. B. stand down
  3. C. stand out
  4. D. stand by
  5. E. stand up
Show the answer and explanation

Answer: A. stand in

Phrasal verbs heavily rely on specific prepositions to alter a verb’s meaning. In workplace and hospital environments, when one professional needs to temporarily substitute for or cover the duties of an absent colleague, the required phrasal verb is "stand in". The colleague acts as a temporary replacement, ensuring the outpatient clinic continues running despite the registrar’s delay.

Why the other options are wrong

  • B. stand down: This means to resign from a position of leadership or relax one’s operational posture, which is irrelevant here.
  • C. stand out: This signifies being highly noticeable or exceptionally superior, entirely failing to convey covering a colleague’s shift.
  • D. stand by: This translates to remaining loyal to someone or waiting in a state of readiness, not actively taking over their duties.
  • E. stand up: This means to rise to one’s feet or maliciously fail to attend a meeting, neither of which fits the clinical substitution context.

Key point: Apply the phrasal verb "stand in" to describe substituting for someone in their professional duties.

Question 2 · English language (PGMI) · medium

Over the past decade, the rapid urbanisation of Peshawar has inadvertently created ideal breeding environments for the Aedes aegypti mosquito, leading to cyclic dengue fever outbreaks. While the provincial government has heavily invested in chemical fogging campaigns to eliminate adult mosquitoes, entomologists argue this is merely a reactive measure. Sustainable vector control demands proactive community engagement, specifically focusing on the eradication of stagnant water in domestic settings such as discarded tyres and uncovered water tanks. Furthermore, primary healthcare centres face an overwhelming influx of suspected cases during peak transmission months. To prevent systemic collapse, health officials have introduced decentralised triage protocols, ensuring that only critically ill patients with severe thrombocytopenia or haemorrhagic manifestations are referred to tertiary hospitals.

What is the primary purpose of the passage?

  1. A. To critique the financial cost of chemical fogging campaigns in Peshawar.
  2. B. To discuss the multifaceted challenges and strategies for managing dengue in a rapidly urbanising city.
  3. C. To highlight the specific clinical symptoms of dengue haemorrhagic fever.
  4. D. To prove that community engagement is fundamentally superior to decentralised hospital triage.
  5. E. To advocate for the complete closure of tertiary hospitals during severe disease outbreaks.
Show the answer and explanation

Answer: B. To discuss the multifaceted challenges and strategies for managing dengue in a rapidly urbanising city.

The main idea encompasses the breadth of the entire text. This passage introduces a core problem (dengue from urbanisation), discusses vector control challenges (reactive fogging vs. proactive community engagement), and details clinical management strategies (decentralised triage to save tertiary hospitals). The only option that captures both the public health (prevention) and clinical (treatment) strategies discussed is the one noting the "multifaceted challenges and strategies".

Why the other options are wrong

  • A. To critique the financial cost of chemical fogging campaigns in Peshawar.: The passage critiques fogging as reactive, but entirely ignores the financial cost aspect of the campaigns.
  • C. To highlight the specific clinical symptoms of dengue haemorrhagic fever.: Symptoms are briefly mentioned in the final sentence to explain triage, not as the central focus of the text.
  • D. To prove that community engagement is fundamentally superior to decentralised hospital triage.: The text presents these as parallel solutions to different aspects of the problem (prevention vs. treatment), not competing options.
  • E. To advocate for the complete closure of tertiary hospitals during severe disease outbreaks.: The text advocates for protecting tertiary hospitals via triage, not closing them down completely.

Key point: Synthesise both the environmental prevention and clinical management details to identify a comprehensive main idea.

Question 3 · English language (PGMI) · medium

During his difficult first week in the incredibly busy emergency department, the newly graduated house officer struggled to _______.

Which idiom best completes the sentence?

  1. A. learn the ropes
  2. B. jump ship
  3. C. call it a day
  4. D. cut to the chase
  5. E. burn the midnight oil
Show the answer and explanation

Answer: A. learn the ropes

Idioms are deeply embedded in fluent professional English. When an individual is new to a job, task, or environment and must learn the fundamental procedures, workflows, and basic operational rules, the standard idiom is to "learn the ropes" (derived from sailing terminology). This perfectly describes a fresh medical graduate struggling to understand the complex administrative and clinical routines of a new emergency department.

Why the other options are wrong

  • B. jump ship: This means to completely abandon a difficult situation or resign, which does not fit the context of a beginner merely struggling to adapt.
  • C. call it a day: This means to definitively stop working for the rest of the day, lacking relevance to a continuous struggle to understand a new workplace.
  • D. cut to the chase: This signifies skipping introductions to get immediately to the main point, completely unrelated to a junior doctor’s orientation process.
  • E. burn the midnight oil: While meaning to work late into the night, it fails to capture the specific nuance of learning the fundamental basics of a new environment.

Key point: Use the idiom "learn the ropes" to describe learning the fundamental basics and routines of a new job.

Question 4 · English language (PGMI) · medium

The senior registrar _______ the ward rounds before the consultant arrived on the floor.

Which of the following best completes the sentence?

  1. A. had completed
  2. B. has completed
  3. C. will complete
  4. D. was completing
  5. E. completes
Show the answer and explanation

Answer: A. had completed

When describing two consecutive events that occurred in the past, standard British English dictates using the past perfect tense ("had" + past participle) for the action that happened first. The simple past tense is then used for the subsequent action. In this sentence, the completion of the ward rounds precedes the consultant’s arrival.

Why the other options are wrong

  • B. has completed: The present perfect tense refers to an action extending to the present, making it unsuitable for two distinct past actions.
  • C. will complete: The future simple cannot describe an action that finished before another past event, so it breaks the time sequence here.
  • D. was completing: The past continuous tense implies the action was ongoing when interrupted, altering the intended sequence of completed events.
  • E. completes: The simple present tense violates grammatical rules when describing events that have clearly already occurred in the past.

Key point: Use the past perfect tense to denote a completed action that occurred before another action in the past.

Question 5 · English language (PGMI) · medium

Neither the head nurse nor the junior doctors _______ aware of the revised on-call roster for the weekend.

Which of the following best completes the sentence?

  1. A. is
  2. B. was
  3. C. are
  4. D. has been
  5. E. being
Show the answer and explanation

Answer: C. are

In English grammar, when two subjects are joined by correlative conjunctions like "neither/nor" or "either/or", the rule of proximity applies. This means the verb must agree in number with the subject that is physically closest to it. Here, the plural noun "doctors" requires the plural verb "are".

Why the other options are wrong

  • A. is: A singular verb does not agree with the plural noun "doctors" positioned closest to the verb in this grammatical structure.
  • B. was: This singular past tense verb fails to match the plural subject "doctors" that immediately precedes the verb slot.
  • D. has been: This singular present perfect form cannot accurately follow the plural noun "doctors" in this correlative conjunction phrase.
  • E. being: This present participle form cannot function independently as the main finite verb of a complete independent clause.

Key point: Apply the proximity rule for neither/nor constructs by making the verb agree with the noun closest to it.

Question 6 · English language (PGMI) · medium

The newly appointed house officer was not yet accustomed _______ the rigorous demands of overnight emergency shifts.

Which of the following best completes the sentence?

  1. A. with
  2. B. for
  3. C. by
  4. D. on
  5. E. to
Show the answer and explanation

Answer: E. to

Certain adjectives in English take specific dependent prepositions. The adjective "accustomed", meaning to be used to or familiar with something, is exclusively followed by the preposition "to". This forms a set phrase that precedes a noun, noun phrase, or gerund.

Why the other options are wrong

  • A. with: The preposition "with" is typically used with words like "familiar" but does not collocate with the adjective "accustomed".
  • B. for: Using "for" suggests a purpose or beneficiary, which disrupts the idiomatic phrase expressing habituation to a specific state.
  • C. by: The preposition "by" indicates agency or method, rendering it grammatically incompatible with the psychological state of being accustomed.
  • D. on: This preposition describes surface placement or dependency and fails to complete the standard phrasal adjective concerning habituation.

Key point: Always follow the adjective "accustomed" with the dependent preposition "to".

Question 7 · English language (PGMI) · medium

All sterile surgical instruments _______ by the central sterile services department before distribution to the operating theatres.

Which of the following best completes the sentence?

  1. A. are inspecting
  2. B. are inspected
  3. C. inspect
  4. D. have inspected
  5. E. inspected
Show the answer and explanation

Answer: B. are inspected

When the subject of a sentence receives the action rather than performing it, the passive voice is required. For routine, habitual actions such as daily hospital procedures, the simple present passive is formed using the auxiliary "are" (for plural subjects) followed by the past participle of the main verb.

Why the other options are wrong

  • A. are inspecting: This active present continuous verb implies the inanimate instruments are performing the inspection themselves, which is logically impossible.
  • C. inspect: This active simple present verb mistakenly assigns the role of the acting agent to the inanimate surgical instruments.
  • D. have inspected: This active present perfect verb structure wrongly suggests the instruments have independently completed the action of inspecting.
  • E. inspected: Without an auxiliary "be" verb, this functions as an active past tense verb, making the instruments the active agent.

Key point: Identify when a subject receives an action to apply the correct passive voice construction.

Question 8 · English language (PGMI) · medium

Despite of his severe fatigue after a thirty-hour shift, the surgeon successfully completed the complex procedure without errors.

Which part of the sentence contains a grammatical error?

  1. A. Despite of
  2. B. his severe fatigue
  3. C. successfully completed
  4. D. the complex procedure
  5. E. without errors
Show the answer and explanation

Answer: A. Despite of

The word "despite" is a preposition that means "without being affected by" and takes a noun directly. Adding "of" after "despite" is a common grammatical error caused by confusion with the synonymous phrase "in spite of". Standard English requires either "despite his fatigue" or "in spite of his fatigue".

Why the other options are wrong

  • B. his severe fatigue: This noun phrase is grammatically sound and functions correctly as the object of the preceding prepositional phrase.
  • C. successfully completed: This combination of adverb and past tense verb properly describes the manner in which the action was finalised.
  • D. the complex procedure: This definitive noun phrase correctly serves as the direct object receiving the action of the verb.
  • E. without errors: This prepositional phrase flawlessly modifies the verb, explaining the exact condition under which the surgery was finished.

Key point: Never follow the preposition "despite" with the word "of".

Question 9 · English language (PGMI) · medium

The consultant recommended that the diabetic patient takes his medication exactly thirty minutes before every major meal.

Which is the correct replacement for the word "takes"?

  1. A. took
  2. B. has taken
  3. C. taking
  4. D. will take
  5. E. take
Show the answer and explanation

Answer: E. take

Certain verbs that express demands, recommendations, or suggestions (such as recommend, insist, suggest, demand) require the use of the present subjunctive mood in the subsequent dependent clause. The present subjunctive always takes the form of the bare infinitive (the base form of the verb without "to" or an "s" ending), regardless of the subject.

Why the other options are wrong

  • A. took: The simple past tense violates the mandated grammatical mood required after verbs of formal recommendation or demand.
  • B. has taken: This present perfect form implies a completed past action, contradicting a prospective medical instruction for routine behavior.
  • C. taking: A present participle cannot function as the finite verb necessary for the dependent noun clause in this structure.
  • D. will take: The future indicative tense is grammatically inappropriate directly following a formal verb of suggestion or command.

Key point: Apply the bare infinitive subjunctive mood after verbs expressing formal recommendations or demands.

Question 10 · English language (PGMI) · medium

The respiratory symptoms of the patient were greatly _______ by the severe, unseasonable smog covering Lahore.

Which word best completes the sentence?

  1. A. mitigated
  2. B. alleviated
  3. C. exacerbated
  4. D. mollified
  5. E. extenuated
Show the answer and explanation

Answer: C. exacerbated

The context of the sentence indicates that an external negative factor (severe smog) is interacting with a pre-existing medical condition (respiratory symptoms). The logical outcome is a worsening of the condition. "Exacerbated" is a formal, precise vocabulary word commonly used in medical contexts to describe the aggravation or worsening of symptoms or diseases.

Why the other options are wrong

  • A. mitigated: This means to make less severe or painful, which logically contradicts the negative impact of severe smog.
  • B. alleviated: This signifies providing relief from suffering, clashing directly with the harmful exposure to environmental pollution described.
  • D. mollified: This term pertains to calming someone’s anger or anxiety, making it entirely inappropriate for describing physical medical symptoms.
  • E. extenuated: This means to make a guilt or offence seem less serious, bearing no relevance to describing a clinical disease progression.

Key point: Use "exacerbated" to describe a situation where clinical symptoms or negative conditions are made worse.
