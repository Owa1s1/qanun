Question 1 · Dental public health, epidemiology and indices · medium

A dental investigator in Peshawar conducts an observational cross-sectional study evaluating the association between naswar usage and oral mucosal lesions. The study compares a group of 150 daily naswar users against 150 non-users. The presence of mucosal lesions is recorded as a binary categorical variable (present versus absent).

Which test compares the proportion of lesions between the two groups?

  1. A. Independent samples t-test
  2. B. Paired t-test
  3. C. Pearson correlation coefficient
  4. D. One-way analysis of variance
  5. E. Pearson chi-square test
Show the answer and explanation

Answer: E. Pearson chi-square test

Statistical test selection depends directly on the level of measurement (nominal, ordinal, interval, or ratio) of the independent and dependent variables and the study design. In this investigation, the independent variable (naswar use: users vs. non-users) and the dependent outcome variable (mucosal lesion: present vs. absent) are both binary nominal (categorical) variables. To assess whether the observed distribution of frequencies differs significantly from what would be expected by chance alone between two independent groups, the Pearson chi-square test for independence (2 × 2 contingency table) is the standard and most appropriate test. If any expected cell count is under 5, the Fisher exact test is applied instead.

Why the other options are wrong

  • A. Independent samples t-test: An independent samples t-test compares the means of a continuous, normally distributed dependent variable between two independent groups, not dichotomous categorical counts.
  • B. Paired t-test: A paired t-test compares means between matched pairs or repeated observations on the same continuous variable, which is inapplicable to independent binary proportions.
  • C. Pearson correlation coefficient: Pearson correlation measures the strength and direction of a linear association between two continuous numerical variables, not proportions of categorical outcomes.
  • D. One-way analysis of variance: One-way ANOVA assesses mean differences of a continuous numerical variable across three or more independent groups, not proportions between two groups.

Key point: Select the Pearson chi-square test to compare categorical proportions between two independent groups.

Question 2 · Preventive dentistry and fluorides · easy

A 42-year-old male bus driver in Charsadda presents for dental treatment. He has placed naswar in his lower anterior labial sulcus 8 times daily for 15 years. The dentist systematically identifies his habit, strongly urges him to quit, and determines that he is motivated to attempt quitting within the next 2 weeks.

In the 5 A's model of brief intervention, which step comes next?

  1. A. Advise
  2. B. Arrange
  3. C. Ask
  4. D. Assess
  5. E. Assist
Show the answer and explanation

Answer: E. Assist

The "5 A's" brief intervention model (Ask, Advise, Assess, Assist, Arrange) is the global gold standard framework recommended by WHO and dental guidelines for tobacco and smokeless tobacco cessation. Once the clinician has Asked about use, Advised cessation, and Assessed readiness to quit within 30 days, the next immediate step is Assist. In the Assist phase, the dental professional helps set a firm quit date, identifies triggers, discusses coping mechanisms, provides behavioural support, and recommends pharmacotherapy (such as nicotine replacement therapy) where appropriate. The final step is Arrange, which schedules follow-up reviews to prevent relapse.

Why the other options are wrong

  • A. Advise: The dentist has already advised the patient by strongly urging him to stop using naswar during the consultation.
  • B. Arrange: Arrange is the final (fifth) step of the framework, which involves scheduling follow-up contact, and must follow after cessation support is provided.
  • C. Ask: Ask is the initial step where tobacco and naswar use status is identified, which has already been completed.
  • D. Assess: Assess is the third step where willingness to make a quit attempt was confirmed; the clinician must now proceed to active help.

Key point: Recognize the sequential steps of the 5 A's tobacco cessation intervention.

Question 3 · Dental public health, epidemiology and indices · medium

A 22-year-old male attends a dental hospital in Peshawar for a routine examination. Clinical charting reveals: 18 and 28 are congenitally missing; 38 and 48 are impacted and unerupted. 16 (upper right first molar) has an occlusal amalgam restoration with secondary caries at the margin. 24 was extracted two years ago due to severe crowding for orthodontic treatment. 36 was extracted due to gross caries. 46 has a sound, intact composite restoration placed three years ago. All other remaining teeth are sound.

What is the Decayed, Missing, and Filled Teeth (DMFT) score for this patient?

  1. A. 1
  2. B. 2
  3. C. 3
  4. D. 4
  5. E. 5
Show the answer and explanation

Answer: C. 3

The Decayed, Missing, and Filled Teeth (DMFT) index assesses cumulative caries experience in permanent teeth. Under standard WHO rules, third molars are excluded unless specifically designated otherwise. When a tooth presents with both a restoration and recurrent caries (such as 16), it is scored as Decayed (D). Teeth extracted for orthodontic reasons, impaction, or trauma (such as 24) are not counted as Missing (M); only teeth lost due to caries are included. Unerupted teeth are excluded. Here, 16 is D (1), 36 is M (1), and 46 is F (1), yielding a total DMFT of 3.

Why the other options are wrong

  • A. 1: A score of 1 accounts only for the single decayed or filled tooth, omitting the other valid components.
  • B. 2: A score of 2 fails to count either the filled 46 or the extracted 36.
  • D. 4: A score of 4 erroneously includes tooth 24, which was extracted for orthodontic reasons rather than caries.
  • E. 5: A score of 5 erroneously includes both the orthodontically extracted tooth and one of the unerupted third molars.

Key point: Calculate DMFT accurately by applying exclusion rules for orthodontic extractions and unerupted third molars.

Question 4 · Preventive dentistry and fluorides · medium

A 3-year-old boy in Swat presents with early enamel demineralisation on the labial surfaces of his primary maxillary incisors. His mother is concerned about caries progression. The dentist applies 5% sodium fluoride varnish (22,600 ppm fluoride) directly to the dried teeth.

Which post-operative instruction should the dentist give the mother?

  1. A. Avoid eating soft non-abrasive foods for 24 hours
  2. B. Withhold toothbrushing until the next morning
  3. C. Drink citrus juices immediately to accelerate setting
  4. D. Fast from all liquids and solid nutrition for 6 hours
  5. E. Rinse vigorously with chlorhexidine solution immediately
Show the answer and explanation

Answer: B. Withhold toothbrushing until the next morning

Sodium fluoride varnish (5% NaF, 22,600 ppm fluoride) sets rapidly when exposed to saliva, forming a thin resinous film adhering to enamel. Because it adheres tenaciously, children can drink water and eat soft foods shortly after application. However, to ensure prolonged contact of the high-concentration fluoride with enamel and enable maximum calcium fluoride reservoir formation, the patient must avoid hard, crunchy, or sticky foods, hot beverages, and all mechanical plaque removal (toothbrushing and flossing) until the following morning. Normal oral hygiene resumes the next day.

Why the other options are wrong

  • A. Avoid eating soft non-abrasive foods for 24 hours: Soft foods can be consumed once the patient feels ready; only hard or crunchy foods must be avoided on the treatment day.
  • C. Drink citrus juices immediately to accelerate setting: Acidic beverages should be avoided as they alter setting dynamics and promote premature dissolution of the resin matrix.
  • D. Fast from all liquids and solid nutrition for 6 hours: Water and soft foods can be taken soon after application because fluoride varnish sets rapidly on contact with saliva.
  • E. Rinse vigorously with chlorhexidine solution immediately: Rinsing immediately disrupts the newly adhered varnish layer and removes unreacted fluoride before it binds.

Key point: Identify the proper post-application instructions following 5% sodium fluoride varnish.

Question 5 · Dental public health, epidemiology and indices · medium

A 14-year-old boy from an endemic fluoride area in Tharparkar attends a screening camp. Intraoral examination reveals that all enamel surfaces of his permanent teeth exhibit marked opacity with pronounced attrition on the occlusal surfaces and prominent, widespread brown staining. However, no discrete or confluent enamel pitting is detected.

According to Dean's Fluorosis Index, how should this condition be classified?

  1. A. Questionable
  2. B. Very mild
  3. C. Mild
  4. D. Moderate
  5. E. Severe
Show the answer and explanation

Answer: D. Moderate

Dean's Fluorosis Index (1942) classifies dental fluorosis into six ordinal categories: Normal (0), Questionable (0.5), Very Mild (1, <25% surface involvement), Mild (2, <50% involvement), Moderate (3), and Severe (4). The Moderate category is diagnosed when all enamel surfaces are affected, accompanied by marked attrition on surfaces subject to masticatory wear and frequent, prominent brown staining. The Severe category is distinguished by the presence of discrete or confluent pitting and hypoplasia, giving the enamel a corroded appearance. In the absence of pitting, this patient is classified as Moderate.

Why the other options are wrong

  • A. Questionable: Questionable fluorosis exhibits only slight aberrations from normal enamel translucency, such as occasional white flecks.
  • B. Very mild: Very mild fluorosis involves small, opaque paper-white areas covering less than 25% of the tooth surface.
  • C. Mild: Mild fluorosis shows more extensive white opaque areas, but involvement is confined to less than 50%.
  • E. Severe: Severe fluorosis is characterized by discrete or confluent enamel pitting and a corroded appearance.

Key point: Differentiate moderate from severe dental fluorosis on Dean's Index based on surface wear, staining, and presence of pitting.

Question 6 · Preventive dentistry and fluorides · easy

A 12-year-old boy in Abbottabad is brought to a school dental clinic for a routine check-up. Intraoral examination reveals fully erupted permanent second molars with deep, retentive occlusal fissures on tooth 47 (lower right second molar), with no clinical or radiographic evidence of demineralisation. The dentist applies an acid-etched resin pit and fissure sealant to protect the vulnerable surfaces.

Which Leavell and Clark level of prevention is this?

  1. A. Specific protection
  2. B. Health promotion
  3. C. Early diagnosis and prompt treatment
  4. D. Disability limitation
  5. E. Rehabilitation
Show the answer and explanation

Answer: A. Specific protection

According to Leavell and Clark, preventive measures are categorised into three levels comprising five distinct phases. Primary prevention occurs before pathological onset and includes health promotion (non-specific measures improving general resistance) and specific protection (interventions targeted at intercepting specific aetiological agents). Pit and fissure sealants, topical fluoride application, space maintainers, and wearing athletic mouthguards are classic dental examples of specific protection under primary prevention. Secondary prevention comprises early diagnosis and prompt treatment (restorations for small cavitated lesions) and disability limitation (pulpotomy or root canal treatment to prevent extraction). Tertiary prevention consists of rehabilitation to restore lost function and form.

Why the other options are wrong

  • B. Health promotion: Health promotion involves non-specific general measures such as dietary guidance or tooth-brushing instruction, rather than applying an active physical barrier to a specific susceptible anatomical site.
  • C. Early diagnosis and prompt treatment: Early diagnosis and prompt treatment represents secondary prevention for existing disease, such as restoring an incipient carious lesion, which this healthy, intact tooth does not possess.
  • D. Disability limitation: Disability limitation aims to arrest advanced pathology to prevent systemic or local complications, such as pulp capping or endodontic treatment, neither of which applies to this sound tooth.
  • E. Rehabilitation: Rehabilitation is tertiary prevention aimed at replacing missing structures to restore mastication and aesthetics, such as bridges, partial dentures, or implants, which are unnecessary here.

Key point: Identify the application of pit and fissure sealants as specific protection under primary prevention in the Leavell and Clark model.

Question 7 · Dental public health, epidemiology and indices · easy

At a community dental camp in a rural union council of Swabi, the team screens 240 school-going children aged 12 years. Visual-tactile examination reveals frank cavitated dentinal lesions in 45 children, whereas non-cavitated early enamel demineralisation, subclinical white-spot lesions, and incipient fissure caries are identified in another 110 children who report no pain or awareness of disease.

Which epidemiological concept is best demonstrated by these findings?

  1. A. Spectrum of health
  2. B. Web of causation
  3. C. Multifactorial aetiology
  4. D. Ecological fallacy
  5. E. Iceberg phenomenon of disease
Show the answer and explanation

Answer: E. Iceberg phenomenon of disease

The iceberg phenomenon of disease describes how the visible portion of disease in a population represents only a small fraction of the total disease burden. In dental caries, cavitated lesions presenting to clinics represent the tip of the iceberg, whereas subclinical, non-cavitated white-spot lesions and unrecognised dentinal decay in asymptomatic individuals form the vast submerged component. Identifying both segments requires active epidemiological screening rather than passive facility-based attendance. The concept emphasizes that community health programmes cannot rely solely on self-reporting patients, as the unrecognised subclinical reservoir in the population requires population-level preventive strategies.

Why the other options are wrong

  • A. Spectrum of health: This concept illustrates individual variations in well-being ranging from optimum vitality to terminal illness, rather than distinguishing manifest clinical cases from hidden asymptomatic population disease.
  • B. Web of causation: This model delineates complex multi-factorial biological and environmental interactions causing chronic illness, rather than visualising the ratio between recognised and unrecognised community disease burden.
  • C. Multifactorial aetiology: This principle describes how host, microflora, and carbohydrate substrate interact to cause caries, rather than the epidemiological distinction between diagnosed and undetected community pathology.
  • D. Ecological fallacy: This bias occurs when statistical associations derived from aggregated population-level data are inappropriately assumed to apply to individual persons, which is unrelated to screening distributions.

Key point: Identify the iceberg phenomenon of disease in community oral health screening data.

Question 8 · Preventive dentistry and fluorides · easy

In a rural health centre in Swat, a community dentist uses the Snyder test to screen school-age children for caries susceptibility. A stimulated saliva sample from an 8-year-old boy is inoculated into molten Snyder agar containing bromocresol green indicator at an initial pH of 4.8. The tube is incubated at 37 °C. After 24 hours, the medium has completely shifted in colour from blue-green to yellow.

How is this Snyder test result interpreted regarding caries activity?

  1. A. Caries inactive
  2. B. Slight caries activity
  3. C. Moderate caries activity
  4. D. Indeterminate activity
  5. E. Marked caries activity
Show the answer and explanation

Answer: E. Marked caries activity

The Snyder test measures the acidogenic and aciduric potential of oral bacteria, predominantly salivary lactobacilli. The test medium contains 2% glucose and bromocresol green as a pH indicator, adjusted to an initial pH of 4.8 (giving an emerald green colour). When inoculated with saliva and incubated at 37 °C, acid fermentation lowers the pH below 4.2, turning the medium yellow. The result is read based on the speed of colour change: a change at 24 hours indicates marked (high) caries activity; a change at 48 hours indicates moderate activity; a change at 72 hours denotes slight activity; and no change at 72 hours indicates an inactive caries status.

Why the other options are wrong

  • A. Caries inactive: Caries inactive is recorded only when the Snyder agar maintains its original green colour without any yellow change through 72 hours of incubation.
  • B. Slight caries activity: Slight caries activity is assigned when the indicator colour change to yellow occurs only after 72 hours of continuous incubation at 37 °C.
  • C. Moderate caries activity: Moderate caries activity is designated when the distinct yellow colour change appears at 48 hours of incubation, rather than within the first 24 hours.
  • D. Indeterminate activity: Indeterminate activity is not an interpretation category; the Snyder test follows an established time-dependent colour change scale across 24, 48, and 72 hours.

Key point: Interpret Snyder caries activity test results based on the time elapsed before indicator colour change.

Question 9 · Dental public health, epidemiology and indices · easy

A health workforce survey conducted in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa evaluates the geographical distribution and employment dynamics of registered dental surgeons. The report highlights that although private and public dental colleges graduate thousands of dentists annually, rural basic health facilities face chronic dental surgeon vacancies while metropolitan centres experience severe market saturation.

Which workforce problem mainly explains this rural shortage?

  1. A. Statutory prohibition of female dental graduates from entering rural government employment
  2. B. Total annual dental graduate numbers falling below five hundred nationwide
  3. C. Complete absence of dental colleges outside the federal capital of Islamabad
  4. D. Compulsory five-year mandatory rural service bonding for all private college graduates
  5. E. Severe geographical maldistribution with clustering of dentists in affluent urban areas
Show the answer and explanation

Answer: E. Severe geographical maldistribution with clustering of dentists in affluent urban areas

Pakistan experiences a severe geographical maldistribution of oral health manpower. Although more than 3,000 dental surgeons graduate annually from public and private institutions, over 80% establish clinical practices in major urban cities where only approximately 35% of the population resides. Consequently, rural areas experience dentist-to-population ratios worse than 1:100,000, leaving Basic Health Units and Rural Health Centres understaffed or vacant. Socioeconomic pull factors, lack of rural infrastructure, absence of mandatory rural service bonding, and urban career aspirations drive this concentration, producing an urban surplus alongside profound rural unmet oral healthcare needs.

Why the other options are wrong

  • A. Statutory prohibition of female dental graduates from entering rural government employment: Female dental graduates are fully eligible under civil service rules for rural recruitment; there is no statutory prohibition against female rural appointments.
  • B. Total annual dental graduate numbers falling below five hundred nationwide: Pakistan graduates over 3,000 dental surgeons annually across recognized public and private institutions, so aggregate graduate output is not below five hundred.
  • C. Complete absence of dental colleges outside the federal capital of Islamabad: Dental colleges are established across all provinces including KP, Punjab, Sindh, and Balochistan, rather than being confined to the federal capital.
  • D. Compulsory five-year mandatory rural service bonding for all private college graduates: Pakistan currently lacks an enforced universal five-year rural mandatory service bond for private dental graduates, contributing to voluntary urban migration.

Key point: Recognise the urban-rural maldistribution of dental manpower in Pakistan.

Question 10 · Preventive dentistry and fluorides · easy

A 16-year-old girl in Quetta attends a preventive dental consultation. The dental team completes a caries risk profile using the computerized Cariogram software, inputting her dietary habits, plaque scores, salivary secretion rate, and fluoride history. The software generates an illustrative pie chart displaying five colored sectors that delineate her risk determinants.

In the Cariogram model, which sector colour denotes the "actual chance of avoiding new cavities"?

  1. A. Green
  2. B. Dark blue
  3. C. Red
  4. D. Light blue
  5. E. Yellow
Show the answer and explanation

Answer: A. Green

The Cariogram is an interactive, computer-based risk assessment model developed by Douglas Bratthall that illustrates multifactorial caries risk as an interactive pie chart. The chart is divided into five sectors: dark blue represents diet (carbohydrate intake and lactobacilli); red represents bacteria (plaque amount and Streptococcus mutans counts); light blue represents susceptibility (fluoride programmes, salivary secretion, and buffer capacity); yellow represents circumstances (past caries experience and related systemic diseases); and green represents the actual chance of avoiding cavities. A larger green sector indicates a higher probability of remaining caries-free, whereas smaller green sectors highlight an elevated risk driven by the remaining coloured domains.

Why the other options are wrong

  • B. Dark blue: The dark blue sector in the Cariogram illustrates diet, reflecting the combination of carbohydrate intake frequency, sugar content, and dietary lactobacilli counts.
  • C. Red: The red sector represents the bacterial challenge, reflecting salivary Streptococcus mutans counts and the patient recorded dental plaque scores.
  • D. Light blue: The light blue sector illustrates host susceptibility, based on exposure to fluorides, salivary secretion rate, and salivary buffering capacity.
  • E. Yellow: The yellow sector represents circumstances, incorporating past caries experience and relevant past medical conditions into the algorithm calculation.

Key point: Identify the meaning of the five coloured sectors in the Cariogram caries risk assessment model.
