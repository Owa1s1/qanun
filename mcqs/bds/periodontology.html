Question 1 · Causes and pathogenesis of periodontal disease · easy

A 20-year-old woman attends a private dental clinic in Swat for routine scaling. She brushes twice daily. On examination, a soft cream-colored deposit on the buccal surfaces of 31, 32, 41 and 42 wipes off with gauze. The dentist explains that salivary glycoproteins adsorb to enamel within minutes after cleaning to form the acquired pellicle.

Which bacterial group colonises this pellicle first?

  1. A. Porphyromonas gingivalis
  2. B. Streptococcus sanguinis and Streptococcus oralis
  3. C. Fusobacterium nucleatum
  4. D. Aggregatibacter actinomycetemcomitans
  5. E. Prevotella intermedia
Show the answer and explanation

Answer: B. Streptococcus sanguinis and Streptococcus oralis

The acquired pellicle forms within minutes of tooth cleaning as salivary glycoproteins adsorb to enamel. The earliest colonisers are gram-positive streptococci, especially Streptococcus sanguinis and Streptococcus oralis, which adhere via adhesins to pellicle receptors. Later, Actinomyces species and filamentous organisms join, followed by anaerobic gram-negative rods such as Fusobacterium and eventually red-complex pathogens in mature subgingival biofilm. In Pakistan, limited access to regular scaling allows plaque to mature into calculus.

Why the other options are wrong

  • A. Porphyromonas gingivalis: This red-complex organism appears in mature subgingival plaque, not on the clean pellicle within minutes.
  • C. Fusobacterium nucleatum: This organism bridges early and late colonisers but is not the first to adhere.
  • D. Aggregatibacter actinomycetemcomitans: A. actinomycetemcomitans is a gram-negative organism of subgingival plaque, not a first coloniser of the pellicle.
  • E. Prevotella intermedia: This organism is associated with later plaque maturation and hormonal changes, not initial pellicle colonisation.

Key point: Identify the earliest bacterial colonisers of the acquired pellicle.

Question 2 · Periodontal treatment, surgery and implants · easy

A 28-year-old man attends a dental OPD in Mardan with generalized gingival inflammation. He uses a hard-bristled toothbrush with a horizontal scrubbing motion. Probing depths are three millimetres with bleeding on probing, and there is no attachment loss. He has never received oral hygiene instruction.

Which toothbrushing technique is most appropriate for him?

  1. A. Horizontal scrub with a soft brush
  2. B. Modified Bass technique
  3. C. Stillman technique
  4. D. Charter's technique
  5. E. Fones technique
Show the answer and explanation

Answer: B. Modified Bass technique

The modified Bass technique combines a 45-degree angulation toward the gingival margin with short vibratory strokes and a rolling motion, making it the most effective manual method for plaque removal at and below the gingival margin in adults with gingivitis. A hard brush and horizontal scrubbing can cause abrasion and recession. Soft brushes and gentle technique are recommended. In Pakistan, soft-bristled brushes are available in most pharmacies and supermarkets.

Why the other options are wrong

  • A. Horizontal scrub with a soft brush: Softening the brush helps, but the scrub technique still misses the sulcus and abrades the cervical areas.
  • C. Stillman technique: Stillman technique is mainly used to stimulate gingiva and is less effective for plaque control at the sulcus.
  • D. Charter's technique: Charter's technique is indicated after periodontal surgery or with exposed root surfaces, not for routine gingivitis.
  • E. Fones technique: Fones technique is designed for children and lacks the sulcular focus needed for adult gingivitis.

Key point: Select the modified Bass technique for routine plaque control in adult gingivitis.

Question 3 · The periodontium, classification and periodontal examination · easy

A 35-year-old man visits a private dental clinic in Sukkur complaining of sensitivity on biting with his lower right first molar (46). The tooth has a deep restoration but no periapical radiolucency.

Which feature of the periodontal ligament lets him feel this biting pressure?

  1. A. Mechanoreceptors in the ligament
  2. B. Fibroblast turnover of collagen
  3. C. Osteoclast activity at the socket wall
  4. D. Its rich blood supply
  5. E. Cementoblasts on the root surface
Show the answer and explanation

Answer: A. Mechanoreceptors in the ligament

The periodontal ligament contains mechanoreceptors that detect pressure, pain, and proprioception, allowing the patient to sense occlusal forces and avoid excessive biting. This sensory function protects the tooth and periodontium from trauma. The supportive, nutritive, formative, and resorptive functions are equally important but do not directly convey the sensation of biting pressure. A deep restoration without periapical disease suggests the pain is periodontal ligament-related rather than endodontic.

Why the other options are wrong

  • B. Fibroblast turnover of collagen: Collagen turnover remodels the ligament; it does not sense pressure.
  • C. Osteoclast activity at the socket wall: Osteoclasts remodel bone; they have no sensory role.
  • D. Its rich blood supply: The blood supply nourishes the ligament and cushions load, but sensation comes from nerve endings.
  • E. Cementoblasts on the root surface: Cementoblasts form cementum and play no part in sensation.

Key point: Identify the sensory function of the periodontal ligament.

Question 4 · Gingival and periodontal diseases in the clinic · easy

A 35-year-old male attends a dental OPD in D.I. Khan with a sore and swollen lower labial gingiva for two months. He places naswar in the lower labial vestibule daily. The gingiva in contact with the naswar is erythematous, swollen and has a white corrugated surface. Other gingival sites show minimal plaque and no inflammation.

What is the most likely diagnosis?

  1. A. Plaque-induced gingivitis
  2. B. Necrotising gingivitis
  3. C. Traumatic gingival lesion from naswar
  4. D. Hereditary gingival fibromatosis
  5. E. Leukaemic gingival infiltration
Show the answer and explanation

Answer: C. Traumatic gingival lesion from naswar

Non-plaque-induced gingival diseases include traumatic lesions caused by local irritants such as naswar placed in the vestibule. The lesion is confined to the area of contact and improves when the irritant is removed. Plaque-induced gingivitis follows plaque distribution and is not limited to a single pouch site. Necrotising gingivitis presents with pain and necrosis. Fibromatosis and leukaemic infiltration have different clinical and systemic profiles. Patients should be counselled about the risks of naswar, including mucosal injury and oral cancer.

Why the other options are wrong

  • A. Plaque-induced gingivitis: Plaque-induced gingivitis would show plaque-related inflammation at multiple sites, not only beneath the naswar pouch.
  • B. Necrotising gingivitis: Necrotising gingivitis causes pain, necrosis and punched-out papillae, usually associated with plaque and systemic stress.
  • D. Hereditary gingival fibromatosis: Hereditary gingival fibromatosis is a slow, firm, generalised enlargement unrelated to a local pouch habit.
  • E. Leukaemic gingival infiltration: Leukaemic gingival infiltration is associated with abnormal blood counts and systemic signs, absent here.

Key point: Diagnose a traumatic gingival lesion caused by naswar placement in the vestibule.

Question 5 · Causes and pathogenesis of periodontal disease · easy

A 35-year-old woman visits a dental hospital in Abbottabad with swollen gums that bleed during brushing. She has fixed orthodontic brackets on the upper arch and poor oral hygiene.

Which component makes up the greatest proportion of the dental plaque biofilm matrix by weight?

  1. A. Water
  2. B. Bacterial cells
  3. C. Extracellular polysaccharides
  4. D. Proteins and glycoproteins
  5. E. Inorganic ions
Show the answer and explanation

Answer: A. Water

Dental plaque biofilm is a hydrated community of microorganisms embedded in an extracellular matrix. By weight, water constitutes the largest fraction, approximately 80 per cent, while bacterial cells, extracellular polysaccharides, proteins and inorganic ions make up the solids. In this patient with fixed orthodontic brackets, plaque accumulates around brackets and bands, making thorough brushing difficult. Understanding the high water content explains why mechanical disruption with a toothbrush and interdental aids is essential; chemical agents alone cannot penetrate the biofilm effectively. This is relevant in Abbottabad and across Pakistan where orthodontic patients often present with gingival inflammation due to poor plaque control.

Why the other options are wrong

  • B. Bacterial cells: Bacterial cells are numerous but do not exceed the water content of the hydrated biofilm in the swollen gingiva of this patient.
  • C. Extracellular polysaccharides: Extracellular polysaccharides form part of the matrix but are a minor fraction compared with water in plaque biofilm.
  • D. Proteins and glycoproteins: Proteins and glycoproteins from saliva and gingival fluid are present but are outweighed by water.
  • E. Inorganic ions: Inorganic ions from saliva and crevicular fluid contribute to calculus later, not to the bulk matrix weight here.

Key point: State that water is the largest component of plaque biofilm matrix by weight.

Question 6 · Periodontal treatment, surgery and implants · easy

A 25-year-old man attends a dental OPD in Quetta with necrotising gingivitis. He has pain, bleeding, and a pseudomembrane on the gingiva. He smokes fifteen cigarettes daily and has mild malaise. After initial debridement, the dentist prescribes an antimicrobial mouthrinse.

Which chlorhexidine regimen is most appropriate for short-term use?

  1. A. 0.05 percent chlorhexidine for one month
  2. B. 0.1 percent chlorhexidine for three days
  3. C. 0.12 percent chlorhexidine once a week for one month
  4. D. 0.2 percent chlorhexidine for three months
  5. E. 0.2 percent chlorhexidine for two weeks
Show the answer and explanation

Answer: E. 0.2 percent chlorhexidine for two weeks

Chlorhexidine 0.2 percent used twice daily for two weeks is the standard short-term regimen after periodontal debridement or in acute gingival infections. Prolonged use beyond two to three weeks is associated with brown staining, increased calculus, altered taste, and mucosal irritation. Alcohol-free formulations are available in Pakistan and may be preferred for patients who avoid alcohol.

Why the other options are wrong

  • A. 0.05 percent chlorhexidine for one month: 0.05 percent is too low for acute infection and one month increases the risk of staining and calculus.
  • B. 0.1 percent chlorhexidine for three days: Three days is too short to control acute inflammation after initial debridement.
  • C. 0.12 percent chlorhexidine once a week for one month: Weekly use is far too infrequent; chlorhexidine is used twice daily.
  • D. 0.2 percent chlorhexidine for three months: Three months of chlorhexidine causes significant staining, calculus formation, and taste alteration.

Key point: Identify the standard short-term chlorhexidine regimen after periodontal debridement.

Question 7 · The periodontium, classification and periodontal examination · easy

Premolars extracted for orthodontic reasons from a 21-year-old woman in Gilgit are sectioned for teaching.

Which type of cementum covers the cervical third of the root?

  1. A. Cellular intrinsic fiber cementum
  2. B. Cellular mixed stratified cementum
  3. C. Acellular afibrillar cementum
  4. D. Intermediate cementum
  5. E. Acellular extrinsic fiber cementum
Show the answer and explanation

Answer: E. Acellular extrinsic fiber cementum

Acellular extrinsic fiber cementum covers the cervical two-thirds to three-quarters of the root and contains collagen fibers but no cementocytes. Cellular intrinsic fiber cementum and cellular mixed stratified cementum are found mainly in the apical third and furcation areas. Acellular afibrillar cementum is a thin layer near the cementoenamel junction. This distinction is important when considering root coverage and periodontal regeneration.

Why the other options are wrong

  • A. Cellular intrinsic fiber cementum: This is found mainly in the apical third and contains cementocytes within its matrix.
  • B. Cellular mixed stratified cementum: This occurs in the apical third and furcation areas and contains cells.
  • C. Acellular afibrillar cementum: This is a thin layer near the cementoenamel junction, not the main cervical root covering.
  • D. Intermediate cementum: Intermediate cementum is a thin layer between dentine and cementum near the apex; it is not the cervical covering.

Key point: Identify acellular extrinsic fiber cementum by location and histology.

Question 8 · Gingival and periodontal diseases in the clinic · easy

A 19-year-old male attends a general practice in Quetta with gradual enlargement of the anterior gums for one year. He has epilepsy and takes antiepileptic medication daily. The enlargement is firm, pale pink and covers the crowns of 11, 21 and 22. Bleeding on probing is present where plaque accumulates.

What is the most likely diagnosis?

  1. A. Phenytoin-induced gingival enlargement
  2. B. Ciclosporin-induced gingival enlargement
  3. C. Calcium channel blocker-induced gingival enlargement
  4. D. Inflammatory gingival enlargement
  5. E. Hereditary gingival fibromatosis
Show the answer and explanation

Answer: A. Phenytoin-induced gingival enlargement

Phenytoin-induced gingival enlargement is a fibrous, pale pink enlargement that commonly covers the anterior crowns and is exacerbated by plaque accumulation. It occurs in patients taking phenytoin for epilepsy. Ciclosporin and calcium channel blockers produce similar enlargement but are linked to transplantation and hypertension respectively. Management includes oral hygiene reinforcement, scaling and consultation with the physician about possible drug substitution. Gingivectomy is considered if function or aesthetics are severely affected after plaque control.

Why the other options are wrong

  • B. Ciclosporin-induced gingival enlargement: Ciclosporin-induced enlargement is associated with immunosuppression after organ transplantation, not epilepsy.
  • C. Calcium channel blocker-induced gingival enlargement: Calcium channel blocker-induced enlargement occurs in hypertensive patients taking drugs such as nifedipine or amlodipine.
  • D. Inflammatory gingival enlargement: Inflammatory enlargement would be red and soft rather than firm and pale pink, and is linked to plaque alone.
  • E. Hereditary gingival fibromatosis: Hereditary gingival fibromatosis has a family history and slow progression from early childhood without drug exposure.

Key point: Identify phenytoin-induced gingival enlargement in a patient with epilepsy.

Question 9 · Causes and pathogenesis of periodontal disease · easy

A 50-year-old man from Larkana visits a dental hospital with generalized bone loss and 6 mm pockets around the molars. He has bleeding on probing and has not had scaling for years. Subgingival plaque from a molar pocket is cultured.

Which organism is a member of the red complex and is asaccharolytic?

  1. A. Porphyromonas gingivalis
  2. B. Fusobacterium nucleatum
  3. C. Streptococcus sanguinis
  4. D. Actinomyces viscosus
  5. E. Aggregatibacter actinomycetemcomitans
Show the answer and explanation

Answer: A. Porphyromonas gingivalis

The red complex consists of Porphyromonas gingivalis, Tannerella forsythia and Treponema denticola. Porphyromonas gingivalis is asaccharolytic, meaning it does not ferment sugars, and is black-pigmented. It thrives in deep periodontal pockets and is strongly associated with chronic periodontitis in adults. Fusobacterium nucleatum is a late coloniser and bridge organism but is not red complex. Streptococci and Actinomyces are early colonisers. Aggregatibacter actinomycetemcomitans is associated with aggressive periodontitis. In this 50-year-old man from Larkana, P. gingivalis is the most likely red-complex isolate from a molar pocket.

Why the other options are wrong

  • B. Fusobacterium nucleatum: Fusobacterium nucleatum is a bridge organism in plaque maturation but is not asaccharolytic and is not a red-complex member.
  • C. Streptococcus sanguinis: Streptococcus sanguinis is an early gram-positive coloniser of supragingival plaque, not an anaerobic red-complex pathogen.
  • D. Actinomyces viscosus: Actinomyces viscosus is an early coloniser and is not part of the red complex in this case.
  • E. Aggregatibacter actinomycetemcomitans: Aggregatibacter actinomycetemcomitans is linked to aggressive periodontitis in youth and is not the classic asaccharolytic red-complex rod here.

Key point: Identify Porphyromonas gingivalis as the asaccharolytic red-complex organism.

Question 10 · Periodontal treatment, surgery and implants · easy

A 24-year-old woman attends a dental teaching hospital in Mardan for routine scaling. She is a non-smoker with no systemic disease. The periodontist plans to instrument the mesial surface of the lower left first molar, 36, which has a 5 mm pocket and bleeding on probing.

Which area-specific curette is most appropriate?

  1. A. Gracey 5/6
  2. B. Gracey 11/12
  3. C. Gracey 13/14
  4. D. Columbia 13/14
  5. E. Sickle scaler
Show the answer and explanation

Answer: B. Gracey 11/12

Gracey curettes are area-specific instruments with a single cutting edge and an offset blade, allowing better adaptation to specific root surfaces. Gracey 11/12 is designed for mesial surfaces of posterior teeth, while Gracey 13/14 serves distal surfaces. Universal curettes such as Columbia 13/14 have two cutting edges and a non-offset blade, making them versatile but less precisely adapted to deep mesial pockets. Sickle scalers are intended for supragingival calculus and should not be used subgingivally in deep pockets because their pointed tip can damage the root surface and soft tissue. Selecting the correct curette improves calculus removal and minimizes iatrogenic injury during root surface debridement.

Why the other options are wrong

  • A. Gracey 5/6: This curette is designed for anterior teeth and premolars, not the mesial surface of a molar, so it will not adapt well to this site.
  • C. Gracey 13/14: This curette is intended for distal surfaces of posterior teeth, so it would not adapt optimally to the mesial aspect of 36.
  • D. Columbia 13/14: This universal curette has two cutting edges and can be used anywhere, but it is less specific than a Gracey for this mesial site.
  • E. Sickle scaler: This instrument has a pointed tip and is used for supragingival calculus removal, not for subgingival instrumentation in a 5 mm pocket.

Key point: Identify the correct area-specific Gracey curette for a mesial posterior pocket.
