Question 1 · Dental public health, epidemiology and indices · easy

A 5-year-old boy is brought to a rural health centre in Charsadda for a dental check-up. Intraoral examination reveals a complete primary dentition with no permanent teeth erupted. Teeth 54 and 64 have occlusal cavities extending into dentine. Tooth 75 has an intact amalgam restoration. Tooth 84 has root stumps remaining from severe dental decay. Tooth 71 was knocked out during a fall from a swing six months ago. All other primary teeth are completely sound.

What is the decayed, missing, and filled teeth (dmft) score for this child?

  1. A. 1
  2. B. 2
  3. C. 3
  4. D. 4
  5. E. 5
Show the answer and explanation

Answer: D. 4

The dmft index quantifies caries experience in the primary dentition. Teeth with active dentinal caries (54 and 64) and retained root fragments resulting from extensive carious breakdown (84) are scored as decayed (d = 3). An intact, sound restoration without recurrent decay (75) is scored as filled (f = 1). The missing component (m) exclusively records primary teeth extracted or exfoliated prematurely because of caries; teeth avulsed or lost due to trauma (71) or normal physiological exfoliation are never counted. Therefore, this child has a dmft score of 3 + 0 + 1 = 4.

Why the other options are wrong

  • A. 1: A score of 1 accounts only for the single filled tooth, ignoring untreated cavities and retained root stumps.
  • B. 2: A score of 2 considers only teeth 54 and 64, omitting the filled tooth 75 and retained roots of 84.
  • C. 3: A score of 3 omits either the restored tooth 75 or the severely decayed root stumps of tooth 84.
  • E. 5: A score of 5 incorrectly includes tooth 71, which was lost to accidental trauma rather than caries.

Key point: Apply standard dmft scoring rules to differentiate caries-related tooth loss and root stumps from traumatic avulsion.

Question 2 · Preventive dentistry and fluorides · easy

In a community school project in Abbottabad, atraumatic restorative treatment is being performed on cavitated primary molars. After the infected soft carious dentine is removed using hand instruments and the cavity is cleaned and conditioned, the restoration and adjacent pits and fissures are completed using the press-finger technique.

Which restorative material is the standard choice for the ART approach?

  1. A. Zinc oxide-eugenol cement
  2. B. High-viscosity glass ionomer cement
  3. C. Silver amalgam alloy
  4. D. Direct microhybrid resin composite
  5. E. Zinc phosphate cement
Show the answer and explanation

Answer: B. High-viscosity glass ionomer cement

High-viscosity glass ionomer cement (HV-GIC) is the definitive material for the ART approach. It was specifically formulated to improve mechanical strength, wear resistance, and setting time over earlier GICs. GIC chemically bonds to enamel and dentine by chelation between polyacrylic acid carboxyl groups and calcium ions in hydroxyapatite. It releases fluoride into adjacent tooth tissues, is biocompatible with dental pulp, and has a thermal expansion coefficient similar to natural tooth structure. The gloved index finger, lubricated with petroleum jelly, presses the GIC firmly into fissures.

Why the other options are wrong

  • A. Zinc oxide-eugenol cement: Wrong: ZOE lacks physical strength, degrades under mastication, and does not chemically adhere to enamel or release protective fluoride.
  • C. Silver amalgam alloy: Wrong: Amalgam requires mechanical undercut cavity designs created with high-speed rotary burs, conflicting with hand-excavation ART rules.
  • D. Direct microhybrid resin composite: Wrong: Composite demands etching, bonding, complete isolation, and light activation, which are unfeasible in low-resource ART field settings.
  • E. Zinc phosphate cement: Wrong: Zinc phosphate is non-adhesive, cytotoxic when unset, prone to oral fluid dissolution, and has no caries-preventive fluoride output.

Key point: State the standard restorative material used in atraumatic restorative treatment (ART).

Question 3 · Dental public health, epidemiology and indices · easy

A 35-year-old male attends an oral screening camp in Swat. The community dentist uses a WHO CPI probe to evaluate periodontal status. On probing the mesiobuccal pocket of 16 (upper right first molar), the black band of the probe is completely hidden beneath the gingival margin, indicating a probing depth of 6.5 mm. Subgingival calculus and bleeding on probing are also noted.

What is the Community Periodontal Index (CPI) score for this sextant?

  1. A. Code 0
  2. B. Code 1
  3. C. Code 2
  4. D. Code 3
  5. E. Code 4
Show the answer and explanation

Answer: E. Code 4

The Community Periodontal Index (CPI) utilizes the specialized WHO probe, which features a 0.5 mm ball tip and a black band between 3.5 mm and 5.5 mm. Sextants are scored based on the most severe finding observed among index teeth. Code 0 is healthy; Code 1 indicates bleeding on probing; Code 2 indicates supra- or subgingival calculus or overhanging restorations; Code 3 indicates a 4-5 mm pocket (gingival margin lies within the black band); and Code 4 indicates a pocket of 6 mm or deeper (black band completely obscured). Although calculus and bleeding are present, the highest score (Code 4) takes precedence.

Why the other options are wrong

  • A. Code 0: Code 0 designates completely healthy periodontal tissues with no bleeding, calculus, or pocketing.
  • B. Code 1: Code 1 requires bleeding on gentle probing but no calculus and no pocket depth exceeding 3.5 mm.
  • C. Code 2: Code 2 indicates supra- or subgingival calculus with the black band remaining completely visible.
  • D. Code 3: Code 3 denotes a moderate pocket of 4-5 mm where the gingival margin sits within the black band.

Key point: Identify CPI codes according to probe markings and the principle of scoring the highest recorded condition.

Question 4 · Preventive dentistry and fluorides · easy

A 24-year-old woman presents to a community dental clinic in Swat for a routine check-up. Intraoral examination reveals mild marginal gingival inflammation across anterior teeth with intact interdental papillae filling the entire embrasure spaces (Type I embrasures). Probing depths range from 2 to 3 mm without loss of clinical attachment.

Which oral hygiene aid is most appropriate for interdental plaque removal in this patient?

  1. A. Dental floss
  2. B. Interdental brush
  3. C. Perio-aid
  4. D. Single-tufted brush
  5. E. Wooden interdental wedge
Show the answer and explanation

Answer: A. Dental floss

Primary prevention of periodontal disease focuses on daily supragingival plaque removal. The choice of interdental plaque control device depends fundamentally on the anatomy of the interdental embrasure. According to Carranza and public health guidelines, dental floss is the device of choice for Type I embrasures, where the interproximal space is completely filled by the interdental papilla. Interdental brushes are preferred for Type II (moderate papilla recession) and Type III (complete papilla loss) embrasures. In Pakistan, where interdental flossing rates are low, patient education emphasizing gentle flossing prevents plaque-induced gingivitis and subsequent irreversible periodontal attachment loss.

Why the other options are wrong

  • B. Interdental brush: Interdental brushes are indicated for Type II and Type III embrasures with recessed papillae; forcing them into Type I embrasures induces soft tissue trauma.
  • C. Perio-aid: Perio-aids (toothpick holders) are designed for cleaning exposed furcations or deep concave root surfaces, not for healthy Type I embrasures.
  • D. Single-tufted brush: Single-tufted brushes are indicated for crowded teeth, furcation areas, or distal surfaces of terminal molars rather than intact anterior interdental contacts.
  • E. Wooden interdental wedge: Wooden cleaning wedges require significant interdental space seen in Type III embrasures and cause gingival blunting if used in intact papillae.

Key point: Identify the indicated interdental plaque control aid based on embrasure classification.

Question 5 · Dental public health, epidemiology and indices · easy

A 13-year-old girl visits a dental clinic in Gilgit. Clinical examination reveals a Class II division 1 incisor relationship with an overjet of 5.0 mm and incompetent lip posture at rest. There is no contact point displacement exceeding 2 mm, no crossbite, and the overbite is normal with no soft tissue trauma.

What is her IOTN Dental Health Component grade?

  1. A. Grade 1
  2. B. Grade 2
  3. C. Grade 3
  4. D. Grade 4
  5. E. Grade 5
Show the answer and explanation

Answer: C. Grade 3

The Dental Health Component of the Index of Orthodontic Treatment Need (IOTN-DHC) categorizes malocclusion into five grades: Grade 1 (no need), Grade 2 (little need), Grade 3 (borderline need), Grade 4 (great need), and Grade 5 (very great need). Diagnostic features follow the hierarchical MOCDO convention (Missing, Overjet, Crossbite, Displacement, Overbite). For sagittal overjet, an overjet between 3.5 mm and 6 mm with competent lips is classified as Grade 2a. However, when the same overjet (3.5 mm to 6 mm) is accompanied by incompetent lip coverage at rest, it is assigned to Grade 3a due to the elevated risk of incisal trauma.

Why the other options are wrong

  • A. Grade 1: Grade 1 represents extremely minor occlusal variations including contact point displacements under 1 mm.
  • B. Grade 2: Grade 2a requires an overjet between 3.5 mm and 6 mm with competent lips, but this patient has incompetent lips.
  • D. Grade 4: Grade 4a is assigned when the sagittal overjet exceeds 6 mm up to 9 mm.
  • E. Grade 5: Grade 5a requires an extreme overjet greater than 9 mm, or defects like cleft lip and palate.

Key point: Assign IOTN-DHC grades by evaluating sagittal overjet in relation to lip competence.

Question 6 · Preventive dentistry and fluorides · easy

A mother visits a primary healthcare dental unit in Peshawar seeking dietary counsel for her 10-month-old infant. The child has four erupted primary incisors. She discloses that the baby wakes three to four times each night and only falls back asleep while drinking from a nursing bottle containing sweetened bovine milk. Intraoral inspection shows dull cervical chalkiness on teeth 51 and 61.

Which dietary advice is most critical to prevent rapid carious breakdown of the primary dentition?

  1. A. Eliminating night-time bottle feeding containing fermentable carbohydrates
  2. B. Replacing sweetened bovine milk with undiluted commercial fruit juice
  3. C. Adding raw honey to the feeding bottle to soothe nocturnal waking
  4. D. Diluting sweetened milk with warm tea containing refined sugar
  5. E. Switching nocturnal feeding to an ad libitum formula regimen
Show the answer and explanation

Answer: A. Eliminating night-time bottle feeding containing fermentable carbohydrates

Early childhood caries is strongly associated with prolonged nocturnal bottle feeding or nursing beyond the emergence of primary teeth. During sleep, salivary flow and swallowing reflexes drop drastically, impairing oral clearance and acid buffering. When bottles containing fermentable carbohydrates (such as milk, formula, juice, or sweetened beverages) remain in the infant's mouth overnight, liquids pool around the maxillary anterior teeth, producing sustained low pH and rapid enamel demineralisation. Mandibular incisors are relatively protected by the tongue and secretions from the submandibular and sublingual glands.

Why the other options are wrong

  • B. Replacing sweetened bovine milk with undiluted commercial fruit juice: Fruit juice provides high concentrations of free sugars and organic acids, accelerating enamel erosion and widespread cavitation.
  • C. Adding raw honey to the feeding bottle to soothe nocturnal waking: Honey contains glucose and fructose that rapidly ferment to organic acids, greatly increasing the risk of rampant caries.
  • D. Diluting sweetened milk with warm tea containing refined sugar: Sweetened tea supplies fermentable carbohydrates and exposes developing teeth to stains without reducing the cariogenic challenge.
  • E. Switching nocturnal feeding to an ad libitum formula regimen: Infant formula contains lactose and other sugars that pool around upper incisors during sleep, sustaining low biofilm pH.

Key point: Identify essential nocturnal dietary and feeding modifications necessary to prevent early childhood caries.

Question 7 · Dental public health, epidemiology and indices · easy

A community dental officer conducting an oral hygiene session at a rural primary school in Swabi avoids delivering passive lectures. Instead, she provides each child with a model and toothbrush, guides their hand movements, and has them clean the plastic models repeatedly until they reproduce the modified Bass technique accurately.

Which fundamental principle of health education is being applied in this session?

  1. A. Credibility
  2. B. Reinforcement
  3. C. Known to unknown
  4. D. Learning by doing
  5. E. Setting an example
Show the answer and explanation

Answer: D. Learning by doing

Health education relies on established pedagogical principles: credibility, interest, participation, motivation, comprehension, reinforcement, and learning by doing. The principle of "learning by doing" (active participation) is especially critical for psychomotor skill development, such as manual toothbrushing and interdental cleaning. Didactic lectures yield low retention rates for physical skills. Engaging learners in hands-on simulation with immediate tactile feedback, error correction, and repetitive performance ensures that neuromuscular coordination and correct angulation are ingrained, resulting in sustained oral hygiene competence.

Why the other options are wrong

  • A. Credibility: Credibility refers to the perceived authority, trust, and professional reputation of the educator delivering the message.
  • B. Reinforcement: Reinforcement involves the periodic repetition of educational messages over time to prevent decay of learned information.
  • C. Known to unknown: Known to unknown involves scaffolding instruction by progressing from familiar local concepts to novel technical knowledge.
  • E. Setting an example: Setting an example involves the educator personally demonstrating personal healthy behaviours in their private and professional life.

Key point: Identify the principle of learning by doing in school oral hygiene education.

Question 8 · Preventive dentistry and fluorides · easy

A public health dental officer is formulating a supervised school-based fluoride mouthrinsing programme targeting 8-year-old primary schoolchildren in Gilgit. The regional water supply has an undetectable fluoride concentration, and community water fluoridation is not logistically feasible.

Which fluoride rinse is standard for weekly school programmes?

  1. A. 0.20% sodium fluoride solution rinsed once weekly
  2. B. 0.05% sodium fluoride solution rinsed once weekly
  3. C. 1.23% sodium fluoride solution rinsed fortnightly
  4. D. 2.00% sodium fluoride solution rinsed once monthly
  5. E. 5.00% sodium fluoride solution rinsed every six months
Show the answer and explanation

Answer: A. 0.20% sodium fluoride solution rinsed once weekly

School-based fluoride mouthrinsing programmes are an effective, low-cost community preventive intervention in non-fluoridated regions. The standard protocol for weekly supervised school mouthrinsing utilises a 0.20% neutral sodium fluoride (NaF) solution (approximately 900 to 1,000 ppm fluoride). Under adult supervision, children rinse with 10 mL of solution for 60 seconds and expectorate completely. In contrast, 0.05% NaF (approximately 225 to 230 ppm fluoride) is designed for daily use at home. Weekly programmes are far more practical in schools because they minimise disruption to the academic timetable.

Why the other options are wrong

  • B. 0.05% sodium fluoride solution rinsed once weekly: A 0.05% sodium fluoride solution provides insufficient fluoride delivery when used once weekly; it is formulated for daily home use.
  • C. 1.23% sodium fluoride solution rinsed fortnightly: A 1.23% concentration is used in office gels (APF) and is too toxic and caustic for volume school mouthrinsing.
  • D. 2.00% sodium fluoride solution rinsed once monthly: A 2% neutral sodium fluoride formulation is designed for periodic professional topical painting (Knutson technique), not mouthrinsing.
  • E. 5.00% sodium fluoride solution rinsed every six months: A 5% sodium fluoride formulation corresponds to varnish; rinsing with such high concentrations poses grave acute poisoning risks.

Key point: Identify the standard concentration and administration frequency of sodium fluoride for weekly school mouthrinsing programmes.

Question 9 · Dental public health, epidemiology and indices · easy

A dental hospital in Karachi routinely audits its sterilization protocols. For quality assurance of the vacuum steam autoclaves operating at 121 °C for 15 minutes and 134 °C for 3.5 minutes, weekly biological monitoring is conducted using spore ampoules to verify complete microbial destruction.

Which microorganism is standardly used as the biological indicator for steam autoclaves?

  1. A. Bacillus atrophaeus
  2. B. Clostridium tetani
  3. C. Geobacillus stearothermophilus
  4. D. Mycobacterium tuberculosis
  5. E. Pseudomonas aeruginosa
Show the answer and explanation

Answer: C. Geobacillus stearothermophilus

Biological indicators (spore tests) provide the highest assurance of sterilization efficacy because they directly challenge the autoclave cycle with highly resistant bacterial endospores. Geobacillus stearothermophilus (formerly Bacillus stearothermophilus) endospores are exceptionally thermophilic and resistant to moist heat, making them the universal benchmark for steam autoclaves and chemical vapour sterilizers. If these endospores are killed following an autoclaving cycle (confirmed by absence of growth or colour change after incubation at 55–60 °C), all other pathogenic vegetative bacteria, mycobacteria, and viruses are confirmed destroyed. Bacillus atrophaeus is reserved for dry heat and ethylene oxide.

Why the other options are wrong

  • A. Bacillus atrophaeus: Bacillus atrophaeus spores are highly resistant to dry heat and ethylene oxide gas sterilization, not steam autoclaving.
  • B. Clostridium tetani: Clostridium tetani is an obligate anaerobic human pathogen that is never employed as a quality-control biological indicator.
  • D. Mycobacterium tuberculosis: Mycobacterium tuberculosis is utilized to benchmark intermediate-to-high-level chemical disinfectants, not autoclave physical parameters.
  • E. Pseudomonas aeruginosa: Pseudomonas aeruginosa is a vegetative bacterium that is readily destroyed by low-level disinfection and moderate heat.

Key point: Identify Geobacillus stearothermophilus as the biological indicator for steam sterilization monitoring.

Question 10 · Preventive dentistry and fluorides · easy

A rural primary health centre in a desert sub-district of Tharparkar, Sindh, adopts the WHO Basic Package of Oral Care. The dental facility must provide emergency relief of dental pain, management of acute oral infections, and basic dental trauma triage within an environment lacking piped municipal water and dependable electricity.

Which designated component of this package corresponds to these emergency clinical services?

  1. A. Atraumatic Restorative Treatment
  2. B. Affordable Fluoride Toothpaste
  3. C. Oral Urgent Treatment
  4. D. Community Water Defluoridation
  5. E. School-Based Fluoride Mouthrinsing
Show the answer and explanation

Answer: C. Oral Urgent Treatment

The World Health Organization Basic Package of Oral Care (BPOC) was designed to make oral healthcare accessible, acceptable, and affordable in disadvantaged communities. It consists of three core elements: 1) Oral Urgent Treatment (OUT) - tailored to relieve dental pain, manage acute oral infections, perform first-line trauma triage, and refer complicated conditions; 2) Affordable Fluoride Toothpaste (AFT) - ensuring community-wide access to effective, affordable fluoride dentifrice; and 3) Atraumatic Restorative Treatment (ART) - a biological approach for restoring cavitated teeth and sealing fissures using only hand instruments and adhesive restorative materials.

Why the other options are wrong

  • A. Atraumatic Restorative Treatment: Atraumatic Restorative Treatment focuses on managing cavitated caries using hand instruments and glass ionomer, not emergency trauma care.
  • B. Affordable Fluoride Toothpaste: Affordable Fluoride Toothpaste is an upstream preventive component aimed at ensuring access to fluoride dentifrice, not clinical emergency care.
  • D. Community Water Defluoridation: Defluoridation addresses high groundwater fluoride toxicity and is an environmental engineering intervention, not a direct clinical component of BPOC.
  • E. School-Based Fluoride Mouthrinsing: School rinse programmes are supplementary preventive community schemes, not one of the three designated arms of the Basic Package of Oral Care.

Key point: Identify the clinical objectives of Oral Urgent Treatment within the WHO Basic Package of Oral Care.
